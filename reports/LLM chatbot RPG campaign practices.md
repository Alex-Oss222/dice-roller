# Engine-owned state makes chat campaigns faithful

Yes: a solo tabletop-style campaign with the properties described can be run faithfully in an LLM chatbot, but only in the shape the reader has already built, where a deterministic engine owns the record and the model is confined to judgment and prose. Every fully readable implementation found in the research reaches the same split (engine owns dice, sheets, clocks and history; the model narrates and proposes changes that code validates), vendor documentation for Claude Code confirms the four capabilities the design depends on (read and write files, run Python and git, load a repository instruction file each session, commit and push), and the benchmark literature explains why the plain-chat alternative fails: recall of facts that must be inferred rather than string-matched drops sharply by 16K to 32K tokens, narrator agents contradict established facts in 40 to 68 percent of interactions, models cannot produce fair random numbers, and instruction adherence decays as context fills. The evidence also marks three places where the design is weaker than the research recommends. Its hardest invariants (never hand-edit generated pages, verify publication, validate before saving) are enforced only by an advisory instruction file, and the vendor states in plain terms that such files are context, not enforcement, while hooks are deterministic. Its tenth-turn evidence review is performed inside the same event by the same model that wrote the nine turns, and the vendor and independent literature agree that fresh-context verification outperforms self-critique. And its rule for skill improvement (evidenced use over in-fiction time, no automatic gain) names the gate that published use-based systems require but not the rate limiter every one of them pairs with it. Elapsed time and off-screen world change remain the least studied area anywhere; the design's computed travel rates and scheduled milestone assessments are consistent with the little that exists, but no source validates a method for simulating weeks of off-screen consequence. Roughly a third of the findings below rest on pages the researchers fetched in full (Anthropic platform and Claude Code documentation, Anthropic engineering posts, GitHub READMEs and SRD mirrors, the NoLiMa results table); the rest, including nearly every academic percentage and every AI Dungeon, Friends and Fables, Hidden Door and Character.AI product detail, rest on search-engine snippets because the primary hosts were blocked, and each is marked as such.

## Transcript memory fails long before turn 40, so files under stable IDs are the right store

The design's decision to keep the story in the repository and to load only `context`, `record ID` and `history --turn N` into the chat is the practice with the strongest support in the whole corpus, and the supporting evidence is largely primary. Anthropic's own context-window documentation states that "as token count grows, accuracy and recall degrade, a phenomenon known as context rot", and that curating what is in context matters as much as the size of the window ([Anthropic, Context windows](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows) [fetched]). The measured version of that statement is NoLiMa, whose results table shows GPT-4o falling from a 99.3 percent base to 89.2 percent at 8K tokens, 81.6 percent at 16K and 69.7 percent at 32K when the question shares no literal words with the fact it needs, with 11 of 13 models dropping to half their baseline by 32K ([adobe-research/NoLiMa README](https://github.com/adobe-research/NoLiMa) [fetched]). That is exactly the campaign case: "the prince we hold" must be matched to an event recorded as a capture several turns earlier. A forty-turn transcript at one and a half to three thousand tokens per turn is 60K to 120K tokens, past every effective length NoLiMa measured. Chroma's report on 18 models, seen only through summaries, found the same decline on retrieval and text replication, and its LongMemEval replication found models succeeding on a roughly 300-token focused extract and degrading consistently on the 113K-token full history that contained the same facts ([Chroma, Context Rot](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot) [snippet]). LongMemEval itself reports a 30 to 60 percent drop for long-context models on multi-session chat histories, and names knowledge updates and temporal reasoning as the weakest categories, which is the "captured in turn 3, ransomed in turn 20" situation the design's stable-ID records exist to handle ([LongMemEval, arXiv 2410.10813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813) [snippet]).

Multi-turn play compounds this. Laban and colleagues' study of 15 models across more than 200,000 simulated conversations found an average 39 percent drop from single-turn to multi-turn performance, split into a small loss of aptitude and a large increase in unreliability, because models "make assumptions in early turns and prematurely attempt to generate final solutions, on which they overly rely" ([Microsoft Research, LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/llms-get-lost-in-multi-turn-conversation/) [fetched]). The remedy the design already applies, re-supplying the derived current state every turn through `context` rather than trusting the conversation, is the one this result implies. Vending-Bench adds a caution in the other direction: long-horizon agents fell into "meltdown loops" with "no clear correlation" to the point at which the context window filled, so summarisation alone would not fix coherence collapse and a periodic external check of the record is the matching countermeasure ([Vending-Bench, arXiv 2502.15840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840) [snippet]).

Compaction behaviour makes the file-based choice mandatory rather than merely wise. Claude Code's compaction summary keeps "your requests and intent, key technical concepts, files examined or modified with important code snippets, errors and how they were fixed, pending tasks, and current work" and discards verbatim tool output and intermediate reasoning; after compaction it reloads the instruction file and re-reads up to five recently modified files ([Claude Code, Explore the context window](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/context-window) [fetched]). A world fact from turn 3 is not in that list unless it lives in a file that gets re-read. The vendor also documents that "instructions from early in the conversation can get lost" through compaction and that persistent rules belong in the instruction file ([How Claude Code works](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/how-claude-code-works) [fetched]). Every commercial memory feature is explicitly non-exhaustive: ChatGPT's help centre says memory "does not retain every detail from every conversation" and Character.AI says it "can't guarantee the Character will always use or reference the information exactly as written" ([OpenAI, Memory FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8590148-memory-faq) [snippet]; [Character.AI blog](https://blog.character.ai/helping-characters-remember-what-matters-most/) [snippet]). Repository files in an agentic tool are the only cross-session mechanism the research found whose contents persist byte for byte and which the model can also write under version control.

The shipped narrative products confirm the pattern from the other side. AI Dungeon partitions its budget into pinned instructions and plot essentials, keyword-triggered story cards and a memory bank of auto-summaries, with the required pinned elements capped at 70 percent of context ([AI Dungeon help, What goes into the Context](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/what-goes-into-the-context-sent-to-the-ai) [snippet]). Friends and Fables builds a "Working Context" of scene-filtered lore and memory blocks before each response, and caps memories retrieved per message by pricing tier "for primarily two reasons: costs and speed" ([Fables patch notes, Franz 2.0](https://fables.gg/patch-notes/franz-20-working-context-lore-improved-planning-and-more) [snippet]; [Fables help, Memories](https://help.fables.gg/articles/2838157-memories) [snippet]). The design's `context` output is structurally the same idea as that working context, computed deterministically and without a per-turn retrieval cap. The one thing these products do not solve, and which the design is right to treat separately, is constraint rather than recall: as one anecdotal write-up puts it, "remembering a sentence isn't the same as maintaining a constraint" ([Kenotic Labs](https://kenoticlabs.com/insights/character-ai-memory) [commercial snippet]). Hidden Door, which assembles human-authored plot beats rather than generating plot, still drew a launch review reporting "a lack of basic narrative consistency" with a scene jumping from a car in Kansas to a dusty road ([Ian Bicking, Hidden Door design review](https://ianbicking.org/blog/2025/08/hidden-door-design-review-llm-driven-game) [snippet]). Consistency has to be checked on output, which is what the engine's verification of claimed changes does.

## Every readable implementation moves truth into code, and the benchmarks explain why

The design's Python engine, validating authorization, capability references, coverage, deadlines and ten-turn reviews on every `advance`, matches the architecture of every open-source project the researchers could read in full. Cyb3rRon1n/oracle states that "the server owns all truth. Character sheets, world state, turn order, the campaign clock, every mechanical number is computed by the Python engine", that the model "narrates and adjudicates through tool calls" and "never gets trusted with arithmetic", and that tool calls are requests the engine validates rather than grants of authority ([Cyb3rRon1n/oracle](https://github.com/Cyb3rRon1n/oracle) [fetched]). spencerthayer/gamemaster keeps an "append-only event history" in SQLite, applies state changes and their event records "in one transaction", returns `UNSUPPORTED`, `UNRESOLVED` or `RULING_REQUIRED` rather than inventing results when mechanics cannot be resolved, and holds that "retrieval is never campaign truth" ([spencerthayer/gamemaster](https://github.com/spencerthayer/gamemaster) [fetched]). Sstobo/Claude-Code-Game-Master runs inside the same harness as the design, loads full campaign state from disk before any response, and states the rule "persist before you narrate, every change saved first" ([Sstobo/Claude-Code-Game-Master](https://github.com/Sstobo/Claude-Code-Game-Master) [fetched]). thecosine/open-tabletop-gm "offloads everything mechanical to Python so the LLM can focus on narration and judgment" ([thecosine/open-tabletop-gm](https://github.com/thecosine/open-tabletop-gm) [fetched]). The one caveat the note attaches is that none of these projects publishes quantitative reliability data; the lessons are the authors' README claims.

The design's append-only, hash-chained log with full state per turn and code-regenerated reading pages is stricter than any of them, and it lands inside a pattern that both practitioner tooling and 2026 academic writing now name. elzobrito/conversation-esaa captures turns into "an append-only activity.jsonl" that "serves as the single source of truth", deterministically projects read models such as handoff.md and state.md, forbids editing projections by hand ("all writing passes through the CLI"), ships `verify`, `doctor` and `repair` commands, and refuses to overwrite modified files without `--force` ([elzobrito/conversation-esaa](https://github.com/elzobrito/conversation-esaa) [fetched]). "The Log is the Agent" describes "the append-only event log serves as the source of truth, with the working graph as a deterministic projection of that log" ([arXiv 2605.21997](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21997) [snippet]). Anthropic's long-running-harness post pairs a progress file and feature list with an initial git commit so that "git commits with descriptive messages combined with progress file updates allow the model to use git to revert bad code changes and recover working states" ([Anthropic, Effective harnesses for long-running agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents) [fetched]). No source found describes a hash chain over an agent event log; the design's tamper evidence is an addition beyond documented practice, not a deviation from it.

Why code must own the record is the best-quantified part of the research, though almost entirely at snippet level. NCP-Bench, which puts a narrator agent against a free-form player across 100 environments with checkable commitments, reports that "fact conflicts constitute the most frequent failure mode (40 to 68 percent of interactions)", that the best model reached "only 42 percent survival rate after 20 turns", and that "high linguistic quality does not guarantee commitment preservation" ([NCP-Bench, arXiv 2608.08160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08160) [snippet]). ConStory-Bench found consistency errors "most common in factual and temporal dimensions", clustering in the middle of long narratives, and its automated checker found 3.2 times more errors than human experts ([ConStory-Bench, arXiv 2603.05890](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05890v1) [snippet]). On rules, "Setting the DC" scored models as tool-grounded referees on state tracking and function usage ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=3Op7kJOvaD) [snippet]), and "Large Language Models Are Bad Dice Players" reports a 7 percent median pass rate for batch generation and 10 of 11 models "passing none of the distributions" on stateless requests ([arXiv 2601.05414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05414) [snippet]). Practitioner accounts converge on the same point at lower evidence: an untamed chat model "narrates beautifully but fudges every roll in your favor" ([BladeRPG](https://www.bladerpg.com/en/chatgpt-dm/) [snippet]), and "when you ask one model pass to narrate the scene and also record what changed in the world, the text looks fine while the underlying state desynchronizes" ([DEV Community, Gemma4 Dungeon Master Companion](https://dev.to/nickc672/the-gemma4-dungeon-master-companion-7jh) [snippet]). The academic framing is identical: an LLM GM "is prone to going off the rails with respect to game rules and flow" and the proposed fix is function calling that keeps the narrative "consistent with the game rules and state" ([arXiv 2409.06949](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06949v1) [snippet]).

One limit of the engine deserves stating because the design already acknowledges it in the phrase "code checks required evidence, not its truth". Constrained decoding and strict tools guarantee syntactic validity only. Anthropic's documentation lists the guarantees as "always valid", "type safe" and "reliable", and lists what is unsupported (numeric and string-length constraints, recursion), with the advice to re-validate against the full schema client-side ([Anthropic, Structured outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs) [fetched]). A Condition number that does not follow from the scene, or a capability reference that is valid but not the one that causally governs the action, passes every schema. That residue is what the human judgment sections below are about.

## Advisory instructions decay, and the design enforces its hardest rules only by prose

Here the evidence warns, and the warning is primary. The design relies on AGENTS.md to forbid hand-editing generated sheets and reading pages, to require validation and rendering after mutations, and to require verified publication. Claude Code's documentation states that instruction files are "loaded at the start of every conversation. Claude treats them as context, not enforced configuration. To block an action regardless of what Claude decides, use a PreToolUse hook instead" ([Claude Code, memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory) [fetched]). The best-practices page repeats it: "unlike CLAUDE.md instructions which are advisory, hooks are deterministic and guarantee the action happens", a Stop hook "runs your check as a script and blocks the turn from ending until it passes" (overridden after eight consecutive blocks), and a PreToolUse hook exiting with code 2 blocks the call even if JSON output says allow ([Claude Code, best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) [fetched]; [Claude Code, hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks) [fetched]). The repository has no `.claude` directory and no hooks; every invariant is a sentence the model may or may not honour on a given turn.

The vendor also documents the specific ways prose rules fail. "LLM performance degrades as context fills. When the context window is getting full, Claude may start 'forgetting' earlier instructions or making more mistakes." "Claude stops when the work looks done. Without a check it can run, 'looks done' is the only signal available, and you become the verification loop." And the recommended fix is a check "that produces a pass or fail", with the model made to "show evidence rather than asserting success: the test output, the command it ran and what it returned" ([Claude Code, best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) [fetched]). A user-filed issue, closed as stale with no maintainer response, claims that rules "correctly applied at the start of a session" are "effectively gone" by turn 20 even when still in context, and that "rules under 200 lines still fade" ([anthropics/claude-code issue #80873](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/80873) [fetched, unconfirmed user report]). Independent measurement at snippet level points the same way: IFScale found even the best models reach only 68 percent accuracy at 500 simultaneous instructions, with a bias toward earlier instructions ([IFScale, arXiv 2507.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538) [snippet]). The design's instruction load (a 37-line AGENTS.md plus roughly 580 lines across four rules files loaded on demand) is well below that density and follows the vendor's advice to keep only universal rules resident and move procedures to on-demand documents ([Claude Code, memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory) [fetched]). One snippet-level factorial study of 1,650 Claude Code sessions reportedly found that length, position and contradiction made no measurable difference while the mere existence of a file raised adherence from 0 of 524 to 67.7 percent, which contradicts the vendor's length claim; the note records the disagreement without resolving it ([arXiv 2605.10039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.10039) [snippet]). Either way, structure alone does not fix mid-session drift; deterministic checks must carry the invariants that matter.

Two mechanical details of the harness affect the design directly. Claude Code reads AGENTS.md as project instructions only when no CLAUDE.md exists in or above the working directory, which is the repository's current state, and a CLAUDE.md that merely tells Claude to read AGENTS.md is unreliable because "Claude sees AGENTS.md only if it decides to open the file" ([Claude Code, memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory) [fetched]). Codex silently truncates combined instruction files at 32 KiB ([openai/codex issue #7138](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/7138) [fetched]), so if the contract is ever run under another agent the file must stay small. And publication is a shell action, not a guarantee: cloud sessions "stop after a period of inactivity and the session's VM is reclaimed", background shell commands "aren't restored", and the cloud VM "clones your current directory's GitHub remote at your current branch, not your local checkout, so push first if you have local commits" ([Claude Code in the cloud](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web) [fetched]). The design's rule that "a local write alone is not an upload" and its idempotent retry keyed on hash, turn and request ID are the right responses; the research adds that the check should be a command whose output the model must show, not an assertion.

The design's resume protocol is confirmed rather than warned against. Anthropic's harness has each session "read the git logs and progress files to get up to speed" and run a cheap end-to-end check to see whether the app "had been left in a broken state", describing sessions as "engineers working in shifts, where each new engineer arrives with no memory of what happened on the previous shift" ([Anthropic, Effective harnesses](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents) [fetched]). ESAA's protocol is "read first handoff.md", consult the other projections "only for gaps" ([elzobrito/conversation-esaa](https://github.com/elzobrito/conversation-esaa) [fetched]). The memory tool's auto-injected instruction is blunter: "ASSUME INTERRUPTION: Your context window might be reset at any moment, so you risk losing any progress that is not recorded in your memory directory" ([Anthropic, Memory tool](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool) [fetched]). `context` validating the full chain internally and printing head hash, turn, pending decision and active obligations is that pattern.

## Adjudication without dice rests on the narrator, so pre-commit and review from outside

The design deliberately has no dice: the GM judges causally from the narrowest governing abilities, fixed before the scene is written, with no plot protection, automatic success or invented rolls. The research supports the pre-commitment half of that and warns about the judgment half. Sycophancy is established across every frontier assistant as a training artefact ([Sharma et al., arXiv 2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548) [snippet]), with SycEval reporting a 58 percent capitulation rate and ELEPHANT reporting face preservation 45 points above human levels ([SycEval, arXiv 2502.08177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08177) [snippet]; [ELEPHANT, arXiv 2505.13995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13995) [snippet]); Anthropic reports its newest models are its least sycophantic and that a joint exercise with OpenAI still found "concerning forms of sycophancy" in models from both developers ([Anthropic, Protecting the wellbeing of our users](https://www.anthropic.com/news/protecting-well-being-of-users) [snippet]). In RPG terms the vendor-level evidence is Latitude's Wayfarer model card: "AI Dungeon players have repeatedly noted that modern AI models are too nice, never letting them fail or die", so the model was trained so that "failure is frequent and plot armor does not exist" ([Hugging Face, Wayfarer-2-12B](https://huggingface.co/LatitudeGames/Wayfarer-2-12B) [snippet]). No study measures an LLM GM's contest outcomes against a rules-engine baseline; the note is explicit that this "should have lost" rate is a gap.

The mitigations with evidence are the ones that remove the decision from the persuadable pass. Fixing the governing capability, preparation, opposition and risk in `adjudication` before the prose is written is the design's version of Blades in the Dark setting position and effect from the fiction before any roll, where "the Occult gang is Tier III. Thorn is outclassed in quality, so her effect will be limited on the lock" ([Blades in the Dark SRD mirror](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md) [fetched]). The consistent lesson across Blades, Ironsworn's rank-throttled progress and percentile systems' opposed rolls is that NPC competence is a number or grade that constrains the PC's best case ([Starforged SRD, Quest moves](https://rsek.github.io/starforged-srd/moves/quest) [snippet]). Solo practitioners warn that PC-only resolution with null failures removes opposition; "a failed roll should not simply mean 'nothing happens'" ([Wilona's Cave](https://wilonascave.substack.com/p/why-solo-rpg-combat-often-falls-flat) [snippet]), and NPCs need "an ordered sequence of goals and conditions" and explicit knowledge boundaries to act rather than react ([CESCG 2025](https://cescg.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/A-Quest-for-Information-Enhancing-Game-Based-Learning-with-LLM-Driven-NPCs-2.pdf) [snippet]; [Ian Bicking, Roleplaying driven by an LLM](https://ianbicking.org/blog/2024/04/roleplaying-by-llm) [snippet]). The design's world and relationship records under stable IDs are where such NPC goals belong; the research does not say they must be a separate module, only that opposition needs a recorded source.

The design's ten-turn evidence review is where the warning is sharpest. AGENTS.md requires the review "in the same event" and retrieval of the preceding nine turns, which means the model that wrote those turns grades them. Anthropic's Fable 5 guidance states that "separate, fresh-context verifier subagents tend to outperform self-critique" ([Anthropic, Prompting Claude Fable 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5) [fetched]), the best-practices page recommends "a fresh model try to refute the result, so the agent doing the work isn't the one grading it" while cautioning that "a reviewer prompted to find gaps will usually report some, even when the work is sound" ([Claude Code, best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) [fetched]), and the Opus 5 guidance says instructed self-rechecks "compound and add cost without improving results" ([Anthropic, Prompting Claude Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5) [fetched]). Independent work finds intrinsic self-correction "often fails to improve and can degrade reasoning accuracy" ([Huang et al., arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798) [snippet]) and a 2026 paper reports models "confidently catch and repair errors in external content [but] routinely fail to identify identical errors in their own reasoning traces" ([arXiv 2606.05976](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05976v1) [snippet]). Judge-bias research recommends binary verdicts, one criterion per call, and a minimal, order-randomised, length-neutral view of the claim ([Patronus, LLM as a judge](https://www.patronus.ai/llm-testing/llm-as-a-judge) [snippet]; [CALM, arXiv 2410.02736](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02736) [snippet]). Nothing in the design prevents the review from being produced by a subagent that has not seen the nine turns' prose, given only the structured records ("capability X at rating Y attempted Z; outcome claimed W") and asked for correctness gaps only; the event contract is unchanged, only who writes the review changes.

The agency rule "do not choose the PC's action" has practitioner support and a vendor-shaped phrasing. Practitioner guides report that "leaving out agency rules causes the AI to play your character for you within a dozen messages" and name "narration drift", the model "fast-forwarding through scenes the player should be playing", as the most common failure ([Arcanum RPGs, What is LLM roleplay](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/what-is-llm-roleplay/) [commercial snippet]). No controlled study isolates the effect of such rules. The vendor's checkpoint rule for Fable 5, "pause for the user only when the work genuinely requires them ... ask and end the turn, rather than ending on a promise" ([Anthropic, Prompting Claude Fable 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5) [fetched]), is the same scope rule phrased positively, and the design's requirement to record a real `next_decision` or null is its concrete form.

## Time, off-screen change and advancement are where the evidence is thinnest

The reader should know that nobody has validated a method for what the design does with elapsed time. The note states flatly that no paper or product document evaluates elapsed-time or off-screen-event realism in LLM campaigns and that no product documents a time model. What exists is a hazard measurement and a set of published tabletop mechanisms. NCP-Bench found that on requests to "skip to the ending" or make narrative jumps, "many LLM agents respond by implicitly retroactively rewriting past events or by allowing impossible jumps, producing text that sounds plausible but contradicts the interaction history" ([NCP-Bench, arXiv 2608.08160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08160) [snippet]), and ConStory-Bench places timeline and plot logic among the two dominant error categories ([ConStory-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05890v1) [snippet]). The design's rules that time is computed from sourced party profiles, that lookups consume no fictional time, and that a five-year proposal does not authorise skipping the meeting are the right response to that hazard: time is an input the engine supplies, never something the narrator estimates. Interactive-drama research stamps scenes with explicit clock times and gives a gamemaster role the job of "describing how time advances" ([ACL 2024 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.196.pdf) [snippet]), and a function-calling GM exposes time-limited progression as functions so the model cannot advance time freely ([arXiv 2409.06949](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06949v1) [snippet]).

For off-screen events, the tabletop evidence says they should come from scheduled mechanisms rather than the narrator's improvisation. Blades ticks faction clocks during downtime so the city advances while the crew rests, and long-term projects are eight-segment clocks chained for longer work ([Blades SRD mirror](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md) [fetched]). Pendragon's Winter Phase is a nine-step annual review covering experience rolls, aging with statistics lost on failure, economic circumstances, family events and one training choice ([DavidTPate/pendragon](https://github.com/DavidTPate/pendragon) [fetched, edition unstated]). Mythic GME does bookkeeping at the end of every scene, updating threads and characters lists and weighting recently relevant entries so they recur ([Mythic GME 2e Adventure Lists](https://www.scribd.com/document/693163042/Mythic-GME-2e-Adventure-Lists) [snippet]). The design's 30-day ordered milestones and tenth-turn reviews sit between Blades' per-downtime and Ars Magica's per-season cadence and are consistent with all of them; Pendragon's checklist shape (experience, aging and condition, economics, relationships, off-screen events) is the closest published template for what a milestone assessment should cover. Generative Agents remains the reference for NPC activity during absence, with daily plans decomposed into timed sub-goals and a memory stream scored by recency, importance and relevance ([arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442) [snippet, 2023]); the design does not need that machinery, but its world records should give major NPCs recorded goals so that an assessment has something to advance.

On skill improvement the evidence issues a specific warning. Every published use-based system pairs a gate with a rate limiter and most add diminishing returns. Burning Wheel requires logged counts of routine, difficult and challenging tests and lets logged practice hours mark a test ([Burning Wheel Procedural Guide](http://questingblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Burning-Wheel-Procedural-Guide-V2.pdf) [snippet]). Pendragon checks a box only for "significant experience" during an adventure, rolls a d20 over the current value at winter, and states that checks "are intended to be very difficult to gain, and are not automatic whenever a success is achieved" ([MK RPG Club, Pendragon winter phase](https://www.mk-rpg.org.uk/Pendragon_winter_phase_summary) [snippet]). Mythras dispenses experience rolls only at the end of a scenario or after two or three sessions and makes higher skills harder to raise ([Mythras SRD](https://github.com/raleel/mythras-srd/blob/main/0004_Game_System.md) [snippet]). Traveller makes weeks of training grow with total skill and allows one skill per week; Ars Magica allows one advancement activity per season; Blades allows each track to be trained once per downtime ([Traveller SRD](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/skills/) [snippet]; [Project Redcap, ArM5 ch. 10](https://www.redcap.org/page/Ars_Magica_5E_Standard_Edition,_Chapter_Ten:_Long-Term_Events) [snippet]; [Blades SRD mirror](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md) [fetched]). The design states the gate ("learned ability with evidence", "no automatic skill gain") but the contract text does not state a per-interval cap or a rising cost, and the note's inference is that a home-grown time-plus-use rule lacking one of those two families "will drift toward free advancement". Gygax's dictum that "you can not have a meaningful campaign if strict time records are not kept" because healing, level gain and language learning all consume tracked time is the older statement of the same principle ([Seed of Worlds quoting AD&D DMG p. 37](https://seedofworlds.blogspot.com/2024/03/tracking-strict-time-records-in-campaign.html) [snippet]).

On canon divergence the evidence is thin and anecdotal. The only report of a model pulling play back toward source material is a commercial reviewer's observation that Gemini "breaks canon by retracting events that were established" ([Arcanum RPGs, best LLM for roleplay](https://arcanumrpgs.com/blog/best-llm-for-roleplay/) [commercial snippet]); no source examines alternate-history play against a known canon. The design's practice of recording divergences as durable world facts and re-supplying them each turn is what lorebook and working-context products do for ordinary lore, and NoLiMa's result that literal, consistently named records survive long contexts where paraphrase does not is the reason to word those facts explicitly and reuse the same names ([adobe-research/NoLiMa](https://github.com/adobe-research/NoLiMa) [fetched]).

## Template and prose control work through exemplars and validators, not prohibitions

The design's fixed turn-output template, with the four-row summary, a turn header, prose and an optional Next, and its bans on mechanics, ledgers and engine commentary in the narrative page, are supported by vendor guidance, with one model-specific caution. Anthropic's advice is to "tell Claude what to do instead of what not to do", to match prompt style to desired output style, and to treat examples as "one of the most reliable ways to steer Claude's output format, tone, and structure" ([Anthropic, Prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) [fetched]); for a specific recurring slip it recommends "one complete example of a correct response" with a sentence explaining why it is correct ([Anthropic, Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1) [fetched]). The template file currently contains placeholders and contract notes but no complete exemplar turn; the vendor evidence says one exemplar would do more than the list of prohibitions. The caution is that Fable 5.1 "leans the other way" on formatting compared with earlier models, and "if your prompt contains anti-formatting language, remove it or replace it with a rule that says when specific formatting is appropriate", because a generic anti-markdown block "can suppress structure the content needs" ([Anthropic, Prompting Claude Fable 5.1, Formatting in chat](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1#formatting-in-chat) [fetched]). The four-row summary table is exactly such needed structure, so any anti-formatting language in the narrative rules should be replaced with a statement of where structure is required.

For prose style, the same page supplies a vendor-tested instruction against "mannered prose" that "substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statement", ending "please remove all mannered prose" ([Anthropic, Prompting Claude Fable 5.1, Writing density](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1#writing-density) [fetched]). The Sonnet 5 guidance warns that "prose style on long-form writing may shift" between models and that style prompts should be re-evaluated per model rather than accumulated, and that sampling parameters no longer exist as a lever there ([Anthropic, Prompting Claude Sonnet 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-sonnet-5) [fetched]). No independent study measures the effect size of a style rule; the medRxiv corpus finding that em-dash use rose with LLM adoption (odds ratio 2.92) is the only quantitative style evidence and it is at snippet level ([arXiv 2606.29540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29540) [snippet]). Practitioner lists of banned phrases work by negative example and are unmeasured ([Foreverse slop list](https://foreverse.app/blog/ai-roleplay-slop-words-list) [commercial snippet]).

Two structural observations about `advance.json` follow from the structured-output evidence. First, the template carries the full narrative as a string field inside the JSON. The vendor notes that JSON is "more verbose, and require[s] character escaping" ([OpenAI Cookbook, GPT-4.1 guide](https://github.com/openai/openai-cookbook/blob/main/examples/gpt4-1_prompting_guide.ipynb) [fetched]), and the researchers' inference is that long free-text fields inside JSON are where escaping errors and truncation land and where the contested Tam et al. results on reasoning inside constrained formats apply ([structure-gen updates.md](https://github.com/appier-research/structure-gen/blob/main/updates.md) [fetched]). Since the model writes the file with a tool rather than through constrained decoding, truncation is less of a risk than escaping; either way the engine should reject a malformed file with the specific violation and the retry should not require rewriting the accepted prose. Second, the design's separation of structured adjudication from rendered prose is the vendor's prompt-chaining pattern with "programmatic gates" between steps ([Anthropic, Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents) [fetched]), but the note is explicit that no study measures whether a decide-then-narrate split improves mechanics-versus-narration consistency; it is widely recommended and unproven.

Adherence to the template is measurable by code in the IFEval tradition: verifiable rules checked programmatically (required sections, forbidden tokens, counts) rather than by a model judge ([google-research/instruction_following_eval](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/instruction_following_eval) [fetched]). The engine already renders and validates the record; whether it also checks the rendered page for exactly one summary table, one turn header, no extra headings, no hash or ledger strings and no capability numbers is the remaining gap, and it is the cheapest deterministic check available.

## Practices worth adopting, ranked by evidence strength

The reader asked for practices without invented process. The table lists only changes the research supports, ordered by the strength of the evidence behind each, and marks what kind of evidence it is. Two practices the research discusses are deliberately not recommended. Per-turn reminder injection of the most violated rules has vendor support in API settings through turn-scoped system messages ([Anthropic, Mid-conversation system messages](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/mid-conversation-system-messages) [fetched]) but no independent measurement, and in Claude Code the same effect is available through a hook, which item 1 already covers. Banned-phrase lists for prose are unmeasured and the vendor's definitional anti-pattern paragraph is the better-supported instrument.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from whether a chatbot can run this campaign to what the chatbot is for. Once the record, the clock, the validation and the publication live in code, the model's remaining job is the one no benchmark can replace and none has scored: judging what a character of a given competence, preparation and opposition actually perceives and accomplishes, then writing it. Every failure class the literature measures (fact conflicts, temporal rewrites, fudged outcomes, forgotten rules, fabricated status) is a failure of the model doing a job the design has already taken away from it. What the design has not yet taken away is enforcement of its own boundaries, and that is the one place where a fetched vendor statement says the current approach will not hold.

The implication for future work is that the design's remaining risk is concentrated, not diffuse. Instruction decay, sycophantic adjudication and free advancement are three names for the same thing, a judgment made inside one context by the party with the most reason to be lenient, and the countermeasures (hooks, a fresh-context reviewer, a stated rate limiter) are each a few lines. The open research problem, off-screen simulation over weeks of fictional time, has no validated method anywhere, so the design's milestone assessments are as defensible as anything published; the honest position is that their quality will be known only from the campaign's own reviewed record.
