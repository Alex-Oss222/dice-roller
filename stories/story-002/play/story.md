## Opening: Turn 0

The morning is clear and cold. Twenty riders left Winterfell by the north gate before the sun was well up, and the road has climbed since then into the hills, where the snow lies in the shade of the rocks and the grass between is brown and stiff. The holdfast is a low stone tower with a wooden palisade, and the men who hold it have the deserter waiting inside the gate.

Lord Eddard rides at the head of the party on a tall grey. Jory Cassel and the guardsmen are around him. Robb is a little back, on the bay he favours, and Theon Greyjoy beside him with the long bundle of oiled cloth across his saddle that he has carried all the way from the castle. Jon's garron keeps its place by Bran's pony without being told; the pony is small and the boy on it sits very straight in his new wool cloak, his hands tight on the reins. He is seven. He has not seen this before.

The gate opens and two of the holdfast men bring the deserter out between them. He is not what a boy expects. He is old, with a grey stubble of beard, and his black cloak is torn and stained; he has lost an ear and part of a finger to frost, and the fingers of one hand are wrapped in a rag. His eyes go from face to face without settling on any. When he sees the grey horse and the man on it he stops walking, and the holdfast men have to move him.

Lord Eddard dismounts. He does not hurry. He pulls off his riding gloves and hands them to Jory, and Theon comes forward with the bundle and unwraps it, and the greatsword is there, broad as a hand and longer than Bran is tall, with the dark ripples in the steel that mark it as Valyrian. Theon holds it out across his arms. Lord Eddard takes it by the hilt.

The holdfast men have set a stump of ironwood in the yard, cut level. They walk the deserter to it. He goes down on his knees when they push him and puts his head on the wood himself, and one of them draws his hair aside so the neck is bare. He is saying something, low and fast, over and over. It is not a plea. The words are the same words each time and the men nearest him look at one another and do not answer.

Lord Eddard asks him his name and whether he knows the sentence. The old man says that he saw the white walkers, that they were there, that he was not lying. He says it to the stump. Lord Eddard says that a man of the Night's Watch who deserts his post forfeits his life, that he is sworn to give the king's justice, and that the sentence is death. He says it plainly, in the same voice he uses in his own hall, so that every man in the yard can hear it.

Robb has gone still on his horse. Theon is grinning, and stops when Jory looks at him. Bran's pony stamps and blows and the boy's hands on the reins do not move. Jon's garron stands close enough that the boy's stirrup is a hand's breadth from his own.

Lord Eddard sets his feet. He lifts Ice in both hands and the blade catches the light along its whole length, and the men who are standing too near the stump step back from it.
