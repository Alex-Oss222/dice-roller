# Jon Snow: background and the reasoning behind the ratings

The sheet's Experience and Disposition sections are the description; this note says why the numbers are what they are, and which Jon they describe.

## Book Jon, not show Jon

The sheet follows the novels. The television series made Jon older, taciturn and stoic, a swordsman first, with the wit, the pride and the sulking cut away, and gave him "I don't want it" where the book's Jon has dreamed of Winterfell since he was small. At the start of *A Game of Thrones* Jon is fourteen and acts it. He is observant and quick, dry-witted when he lets it out, proud, sullen when hurt and quick to temper when his birth is touched. By his own account he is the better sword of the two brothers and Robb the stronger lance, and the master of horse rates his riding with anyone in the castle.

## Method

Inference from the record as it stands on the morning the story opens, scaled to a fourteen-year-old. Later chapters are used for standing traits and for facts about his past, such as the Winterfell dream; nothing that happens after this morning is on the sheet.

- Longsword 4 and the Martial domain at 3. Daily drill under a master-at-arms; the better sword of the two brothers by his own account; never in real danger. No strong aptitude: the books put the advantage down to training.
- Riding 5. Hullen's judgment as Jon reports it. The Fieldcraft domain stays 3 because Hunting, Forest Travel and Survival are those of a boy who rides and hunts near Winterfell, not a ranger.
- Persuasion 3, Oratory 1, Diplomacy 3. He can move a man who is hard to move with the right argument; he fails with Benjen at the feast, and he has never spoken to a hall.
- History 4 with strong aptitude, Geography 3, Learning 4. Well read beyond his lessons, with the Young Dragon as his boyhood hero. The aptitude is the one forward-looking judgment on the sheet; the later books show him reading history for precedent.
- Detecting Lies 3, Concealment 2, Intrigue 2. Benjen's and Tyrion's observations make him a watcher; his moods show, so hiding things stays basic, though he keeps the Winterfell dream to himself.
- Craft 2 and no numeric Stewardship. A boy who keeps his own gear and has never run a household.

Where the books are silent (hunting, wrestling, weapon care, camp craft), the ratings are what a lord's son of fourteen at Winterfell would plausibly have, and they are kept modest.

## Languages: the player's premise

The novels establish only the Common Tongue for Jon. The player has chosen, as a standing preference across their stories, that Jon speaks, reads and writes the Old Tongue, High Valyrian and Low Valyrian fluently. The sheet records that as a story premise with a plausible route (the maester's books, tuition, Free Cities traders in the North), and the setup assumptions say it is not canon. Low Valyrian is a family of regional dialects rather than one written language; the sheet treats it as the trade tongue of the Free Cities in its Braavosi and Pentoshi forms.
