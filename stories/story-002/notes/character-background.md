# Jon Snow — background and the reasoning behind the ratings

The sheet's Experience section is the description; this note says why the numbers are what they are.

The method is inference from the record as it stands on the morning the story opens, not from what Jon later becomes. The novels show a boy of fourteen who has been drilled daily by a master-at-arms and is held to be the best of the boys in the yard, quicker than Robb and less strong; who rides and hunts with his father's household; who has had a maester's schooling; who keeps the old gods; and who has learned, as a bastard in Lady Catelyn's household, to watch and hold his tongue. Everything on the sheet is one of those facts scaled to a fourteen-year-old.

Longsword 4 is the highest rating and the most deliberate. Skilled, not veteran: he has never fought a man who meant to kill him, never in armor heavier than a mail shirt, never in a line. The strong aptitude recorded against it is the one place the sheet reaches past the morning: the yard's opinion of him is unanimous, and that is evidence of talent, not of experience. It changes the pace at which practice pays, not what he can do today.

The Fieldcraft ratings are those of a boy who has ridden and hunted near Winterfell, not a ranger's. Navigation and Survival sit at 2 because he has never been beyond a known road or spent a cold night alone. Learning sits at 3 because a maester's schooling is a real education and he took to it. Diplomacy 3 is the conduct of a great house learned from inside it, with no court in it. Intrigue 2 is not training; it is what a watchful boy has. Craft 2 is a boy who keeps his own gear. Stewardship has no numeric domain because he has never run any part of a household; the two abilities recorded under it are what a lord's son picks up by being present.

Nothing on the sheet is a later-book fact: no direwolf, no raven, no royal party, no oath, no mother.
