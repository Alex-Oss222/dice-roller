## Transfer boundaries

The supplied premise establishes the transfer of Jon's consciousness into Cregan's body. Jon retains his own identity, personality, memories, knowledge, and accumulated experience. He does not become a novice in the disciplines he has practiced.

The following are explicit starting assumptions where the prompt leaves a detail open:

- Cregan's private memories are not automatically added to Jon's. Familiarity with northern history does not supply recognition of every servant or recollection of yesterday's conversations.
- Physical action uses Cregan's present body. Jon retains learned technique and judgment, but cannot assume the reach, conditioning, old scars, modest speed advantage, or exceptionally slow aging of his former body.
- The original premise moved consciousness, not possessions, companions, descendants, or institutions. The player explicitly added one exception in Turn 4: the original Ghost arrived physically on the fifteenth. Longclaw, the Lysene spear, Huginn, Muninn, and the manor have not appeared in 126 AC.

- The transfer's cause, reversibility, and effects on Cregan's original consciousness are unknown.
