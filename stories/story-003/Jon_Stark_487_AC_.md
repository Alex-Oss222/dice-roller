## Background

Jon grew up at Winterfell as Eddard Stark's acknowledged bastard son. He shared the education of the Stark children: letters and sums under Maester Luwin, arms under Ser Rodrik Cassel, riding, hunting, histories, languages, customary law, and the conduct expected in a great northern household. Bastardy affected his place at Winterfell and made him observant of rank, affection, and the difference between what people said and what they intended.

He joined the Night's Watch and rose to Lord Commander before the Night's King attacked in 310 AC. Jon commanded from the beginning of the five-year defense. His own Watchmen murdered him; the Old Gods restored him to life. That return carried an unintended consequence: his aging became exceptionally slow. It did not make him invulnerable, grant rapid healing, or remove old injuries.

In 315 AC, in the presence of the assembled northern army before Winterfell, Jon fought the Night's King in single combat with **Longclaw and an obsidian spear** and killed him. The Night's King's death slowed and disorganized the remaining enemy rather than destroying it immediately. Northern forces spent another two years purging the survivors. Neither Daenerys Targaryen nor the southern kingdoms came to the North's aid; Daenerys won the struggle for the southern throne while the North fought its own war.

Jon became **King Jon Stark** in 315 AC. During sixty-two years on the throne he defended northern independence against repeated southern attempts at reconquest, including assaults toward Moat Cailin, by sea, and through the Dreadfort. The Free Folk joined the kingdom; the Night's Watch was disbanded after its former function ended. Skagos entered peacefully through Rickon Stark's connections there, and Rickon's line became its lords. Jon supported northern fortifications, ports, and trade, including the commercial reach of White Harbor and House Manderly.

From around 325 AC, he directed major reconstruction and the non-generational projects described in the Northern development plans. His rule demanded decisions about food reserves, roads, bridges, ports, castles, law, records, public works, and the organization of labor and money. The complete village-to-village, town-to-town, and castle-to-castle road and mapping network took roughly 120 years, continuing under his successors. His own expertise is in *directing and sustaining such work*, not in personally performing every mason's or engineer's trade.

Allyria Dayne came as Arianne Martell's envoy to request northern assistance against Daenerys. Jon refused another southern war but opened trade with Dorne. Jon and Allyria married and had two children, **Lyanna** and **Robb**. In 377 AC, believing the North stable enough to govern without him, he voluntarily abdicated. Lyanna succeeded him under the Northern succession law. Allyria died in 394 AC, seventeen years later.

Jon has spent approximately **110 years since abdication** traveling, studying, learning languages, meeting people, and returning to his manor in the Wolfswood. He has visited the other regions of Westeros and widely throughout Essos and beyond, including the Nine Free Cities, the Rhoyne, the Stepstones, the Dothraki Sea, Lhazar, the Ghiscari cities, Ibben, the Summer Isles, Naath, Qarth, the Jade Sea, Yi Ti, Leng, and Asshai. He has learned within those cultures rather than merely passing through them.

## Established experience

### Arms and command

Jon has practiced sword, spear, and axe combat for most of his life. Training at Winterfell was followed by service at the Wall, personal combat against lethal opponents, command in the Long Night, the subsequent pursuit of scattered enemies, wars against southern armies, and continued practice after abdication. He has faced differences in armor, reach, formations, terrain, exhaustion, equipment, and the temperament and training of opponents. **Longclaw** remains his principal known sword; he also owns a spear forged in Lys from dragonbone and Valyrian steel.

He has led forces through campaigns where supplies, discipline, intelligence, morale, weather, and divided loyalties mattered as much as battlefield fighting. His command knowledge covers defensive warfare, marching, fortifications, sieges, scouting, communication, reserves, logistics, and the use of different kinds of troops. His post-abdication travels added study of foreign arms and military practices. This makes him an exceptional commander, not an infallible one: he can receive bad intelligence, misjudge an enemy, be outnumbered, or lose men despite sound plans.

### Law, diplomacy, and political judgment

As king Jon dealt for sixty-two years with rival noble interests, Free Folk leaders, claims of land and inheritance, petitions, judgments, appointments, military service, and the practical reach of decrees. He understands feudal allegiance, custom, succession, negotiation, and the need to make settlements people will actually observe. His dynasty's Royal Writ of Succession is an example of long-range institutional design.

He has negotiated with foreign envoys and merchants and learned how different cultures conduct authority, bargaining, courtesy, and refusal. He turned down Dorne's request for military intervention without rejecting Dorne itself, making trade possible where an alliance was not. He can identify competing interests and the political cost of a concession. He need not enjoy manipulation to understand it.

### Governance, building programs, and resources

Jon governed a realm of great distances, difficult travel, independent-minded houses, and long winters. He oversaw the rebuilding of defenses, supply networks, ports, roads, markets, granaries, settlement programs, recordkeeping, and institutions. His experience extends to levies, expenditure, provisioning, auditing, wages and labor obligations, taxation, shipping, development priorities, and succession.

He learned to employ capable stewards, surveyors, captains, craftsmen, merchants, and local lords; to balance rival projects; and to distinguish permanent maintenance from a ceremonial opening. He understands what kinds of information specialists should be asked to provide. This does **not** establish that he can personally survey every bridge, calculate every ship's hull, cut dressed stone, or produce a master blacksmith's work.

### Travel and fieldcraft

Jon was a trained northern rider and hunter before becoming a soldier. Since then he has traveled through the wild North, lands beyond the Wall, and widely different regions abroad, including distant countries that require new routes, interpreters, guides, local knowledge, and forms of transport. He knows how to prepare supplies, choose ground, read trails, manage a camp, judge travel risks, and look after himself without a noble entourage.

He is markedly better acquainted with many regions than a typical traveler, but he does not automatically know every local path, secret pass, or current political danger. Sea travel has made him familiar with the practical demands of voyages without establishing that he is a master shipwright or admiral.

### Education, medicine, and comparative study

Jon's early highborn education became the base for many decades of deliberate study. He has pursued **philosophy, mathematics, medicine, religion, warfare, history, languages, and the higher mysteries**, learning both from texts and from the people whose traditions they describe. His unusual lifespan permits breadth, sustained specialization, and repeated comparison between theory and lived consequences.

He has studied and practiced the treatment of wounds, including cleaning, dressing, observation, recovery, and caring for his own injuries. His knowledge is serious and practical, but his patients remain subject to infection, irreversible damage, and death. His mathematics covers ordinary and sophisticated reckoning as well as its uses in accounts, trade, surveys, supply, and construction. His study of religion and philosophy extends beyond agreement with what he has read.

Jon retains an unusually detailed **autobiographical memory**, particularly for experiences from his own life. It is **not** an eidetic record of every book, map, word, or fact he has encountered. He can forget particulars, misremember explanations, and encounter subjects outside his expertise.

### Magic and the Old Gods

Jon can **warg into many kinds of animals** and **use glass candles to communicate over long distances**. He has studied esoteric traditions far more broadly than his demonstrated magical repertoire. He is not established as a universal spellcaster, a healer by magic, a greenseer, or a person with unlimited supernatural knowledge. The specific mechanics of his longevity, Ghost's longevity, and the ravens' longevity need no additional explanation.

### Personal life and disposition

Jon is more patient, more openly humorous, and more interested in life than the guarded boy he once was. With strangers he is observant, plainspoken, and dryly witty. With friends and trusted company he laughs freely. He can return immediately to sober, disciplined judgment when a situation merits it. He enjoys learning for its own sake and does not require a political objective to make an activity worthwhile.

Two enduring weaknesses are **difficulty accepting praise** and **difficulty accepting loss**. He may discount sincere appreciation or resist acknowledging what cannot be repaired, including the deaths of people he loved. Time and memory have not solved these weaknesses. His patience is not passivity, and his refusal to reclaim power is a choice rather than helplessness.

### Capability adjudication rules

1. Use the **recorded specialty** when one clearly applies.
2. Otherwise use a **broad domain** only where Jon's documented experience reasonably covers the task.
3. Interpret every number in light of the evidence and explicit limits; do not average specialties into a broad rating or treat a high domain as universal mastery.
4. Opponents, available information, equipment, injuries, preparation, location, time, and physical constraints remain independently relevant.
5. Do not turn ratings into fixed chances, die modifiers, target numbers, or guaranteed outcomes.
6. No authority flows automatically from having once been king. Jon cannot order the reigning monarch, other lords, or strangers merely on the strength of a Stewardship or Diplomacy rating.
7. Magic has only the effects expressly established. A score records practice, not a new list of spells.
8. This source record describes Jon before the transfer in 487 AC; CHARACTER.md and WORLD.md govern his current campaign state and the full memories supplied by its premise. He does not automatically know other people's hidden intentions or information absent from his established experience and memories.
9. Ratings can change in later records only if substantial new experience, loss of ability, or clear evidence justifies the change.
10. An unlisted specialty is not automatically a 1. Derive the likely ability from what Jon demonstrably knows and has practiced; leave unsupported expertise unestablished.

- **Lyanna Stark:** His daughter and successor. Their relationship belongs to his family history rather than a continuing royal chain of command.
- **Robb Stark (son):** His second child; a recognized member of the royal bloodline and nominee under Jon's final writ.

- **Huginn and Muninn:** Original ravens and continuing companions; likewise physically long-lived.

## Reputation and legal standing

Jon is remembered in accounts as the ruler who killed the Night's King, preserved Northern independence, and rebuilt the kingdom. The public myth and surviving history are not the same as recognition of the man. Most strangers in 487 AC do **not** identify an ordinary-looking man of thirty as the former king of more than a century ago. He typically offers only the name **Jon** and does not attract constant hero worship.

He is respected as an ancestor by the ruling Stark family but does not claim governing authority over his descendants. His reign ended with **voluntary abdication in 377 AC**; he is not a hidden regent, deposed claimant, rival king, or continuing Lord Commander. The Night's Watch itself has been disbanded.

His Northern succession law calls for each monarch to issue and renew a Royal Writ naming **three bloodline candidates** and one chosen heir. Under ordinary circumstances the chosen heir succeeds; recognized lords of the **entire independent Northern realm**, including Skagos and beyond the Wall, can challenge with support totaling **40%** of weighted noble votes. If challenged, the three nominees stand for election; the chosen heir receives three royal votes, major lords two each, and minor lords one each. Abdication and death use the same procedure. The full legal provisions are a **separate document**, not a governing task Jon still performs.

## Knowledge

Jon personally knows or remembers:

- The Night's Watch as it operated while he served and commanded it, including the betrayal by his own men and the later decision to disband it.
- The Night's King's attack from 310 AC, the duel before Winterfell in 315 AC, and the subsequent two-year pursuit and purge.
- How the North survived without military help from Daenerys, the southern kingdoms, Dorne, or the Free Cities during that struggle.
- The political and military pressures of independent rule, including invasions at Moat Cailin, by sea, and toward the Dreadfort.
- The decisions and tradeoffs behind the rebuilding program he directed, and that later rulers completed generational works begun under him.
- The history of his marriage, his children, his abdication, and the dynasty's succession process.
- Numerous living or written traditions encountered over extensive travel, including foreign languages, philosophy, religion, medicine, martial doctrine, and esoteric study.
- His established warging and glass-candle abilities, and the ordinary limits of his healing and mortality.
- The difference between events he personally experienced, reports he received, and histories interpreted by later people.

Jon does **not** thereby know every secret of a foreign court, every road built after his reign, every page he once read, or what another person currently thinks. His unusually precise autobiographical memory is not perfect factual memory. Current affairs in regions he has not visited recently must be learned anew.

## Duties and commitments

- Care for his private home, possessions, and animals; he deliberately does much of this work himself.
- Maintain the relationships he chooses with family, friends, and other companions.
- Respect the lawful authority of the reigning king and the independence of people who now hold offices Jon once held.
- No established present duty to command armies, run the royal treasury, resolve every dispute brought to him, or seek a new political cause.

## Present aim

Continue living as Jon: care for his Wolfswood manor, remain with Ghost, Huginn, and Muninn, travel when he wishes, pursue serious study, practice skills that interest him, and enjoy friendship, conversation, and ordinary experiences. He is capable of serious intervention if a real circumstance warrants it, but he does **not** seek another crown or another war to justify his continued life.
