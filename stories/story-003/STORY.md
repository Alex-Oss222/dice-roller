## Turn 0 | 126 AC, exact date unestablished | Morning
Elapsed: None; opening situation.

The clerk was still speaking when Jon became aware of the room. There was an account book before him, open at a heading dated 126 AC. The man beside the table wore a bunch of keys at his belt. Jon did not know him.

The body in the chair was Cregan Stark's. Jon remembered his own life through 487 AC, but none of the conversation that had put these papers in front of him.

“Shall I leave these with you, Lord Cregan?”

The clerk pulled the loose sheets together, knocked their edges against the table and laid a narrow board over them. He passed cord beneath the board, crossed it twice and tied the bundle. A split in the cord caught on the corner. He cut that length away and started again.

“That is everything,” he said.

A page came through the door with another bundle. The clerk took it from him.

The man with the keys waited until both had gone. A guard remained outside the open door.

“Your uncle has asked to see you. He wants no clerk present.”

He set a separate folded sheet beside the accounts. It was addressed to Lord Cregan Stark. Bennard's name stood beneath a short request for an audience.

“He and the boys are still confined. I've changed nothing there.”

Jon could remember administering prisons and hearing petitions. He could not remember this man's name, or what Cregan had last told him to do. The steward had brought his own writing case and was taking out a knife to mend the point of his quill.

“He says it is family business. I told the guard I'd bring his request.”

The steward laid the quill beside the account book. He had not sent for Bennard, and the guard had received no instruction to move the prisoners.

## Turn 1 | Third day of the fourth moon, 126 AC | Noon

Elapsed: About four hours.

Jon crossed to the window. The sun stood well short of its highest point. Below, a cart was being unloaded beside the kitchens; two men carried sacks while a third checked the marks against a tally.

“When did you make the last entry?”

“This morning, my lord. The balance brought forward into your new account.”

“And the date?”

“The third day of the fourth moon. One hundred and twenty-six.”

The steward showed him the heading and the dated entry beneath it. By the sun and the household's morning work, it was roughly four hours before noon.

Jon called the guard at the door. If Arra Norrey was in Winterfell, he wanted her asked to meet him at the godswood entrance after the midday meal. The guard repeated the place and time before leaving.

The account book covered twelve estates. Rent rolls lay beneath it, with separate schedules for payments from the northern houses. Jon began with the balance carried forward, then worked through the receipts and charges that supported the steward's reckoning.

“There is no need for another ledger,” the steward said.

Before the next hour had passed, he sent the clerk for the granary book.

They untied the estate returns, arranged them in the order of the main account and weighted the corners. The clerk read each subtotal. Jon added the columns independently and matched selected entries to the receivers' sheets. Paid charges went on one side of the table; demands and estimates went on the other.

The treasury balance was 250,000 gold dragons, reckoned across the coin entered in House Stark's books. The grain account carried 70,777 quarters, each of eight bushels, across the stores charged to the lordship. These were the balances the officers answered for. Checking their addition did not count the chests or measure the grain.

The larger figures belonged to an ordinary year's reckoning. Gross receipts came to 283,632 dragons, including 179,181 expected from thirty-five direct bannermen. Estate expenses took 10,464 before remittance. Household costs, customary works and the Watch's cash gift required another 11,450; the expected Crown payment was 40,975. On those assumptions, a full year added 220,743 dragons.

Jon followed the tribute schedules back to their houses. Manderly supplied the largest share. The five largest contributors together accounted for more than half the demand. Their own treasuries were not included in this balance, nor could their money be spent merely by entering it as expected revenue.

The existing coin could cover the entire 62,889 dragons of ordinary annual charges without fresh receipts, leaving 187,111. No outstanding loan or extraordinary unpaid demand appeared in the schedules put before him.

*Keep the books tight, the larders full, and winter out.*

Ned had said it to his children. Jon checked the grain obligations alongside the cash: the customary Watch gift included two thousand quarters. A promise of silver would not fill those wagons.

By noon he had the balances, the principal sources of income and the customary burdens clear. The summary arithmetic agreed with the supporting entries he had checked. The individual northern houses' private accounts remained their own.

The guard returned. Arra was in Winterfell. The message had reached her, and she would come after the meal.

Jon left the books in their separate piles and went down to the godswood entrance. He reached it before Arra. A guard stood a few paces from the gate while servants carried the last covered dishes across the yard.
