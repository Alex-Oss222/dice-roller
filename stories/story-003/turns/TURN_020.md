## Turn 20 | 129/08/19 | Night
Elapsed: 134 days: fourteen days of preparation, followed by four thirty-day moons.

### The orders and the fortnight

“The kingsroad first,” Jon told Dustin. “A road our supply carts can use behind us. Then the Trident crossings and Harrenhal. We will ask the Freys to keep their bridge, not leave it to march with us. The western lords can defend their own country if we give them reason to work with us.”

The letter to Daemon named the route and the intended departure. Jon requested current reports, a liaison and arrangements for a northern camp near Harrenhal. He offered cooperation without placing his companies under another command. Letters to the riverlords offered payment for provisions, protection of customary holdings and respect for their own officers in return for passage, guides, warning posts and supply agreements.

Couriers left in pairs over the following two days. The Frey packet included approaches to Vypren and Charlton; the others went towards Seagard, Lord Harroway’s Town, Darry, Raventree Hall and Riverrun. Jacaerys carried a shorter copy of the Harrenhal letter south with his articles. The full account followed by road. Jon’s first letter to the Blackwoods asked for their present position; the congratulations followed after his scouts brought confirmation of the fighting and their lord’s death.

The captains’ returns produced a marching establishment of 7,200, not a new levy. Jon put five thousand in the main fighting body, with its reserve under his own direction. Dustin took a leading division. Ryswell supervised the advance screen; Tallhart’s officers worked with the carpenter on the road parties. Manderly’s officers organized cargo and wagon accounts. The remaining duties covered the advance, baggage, rear, headquarters and the small establishment staying at the Moat. Company captains retained their men within those assignments.

“All the wagons are loaded,” one of them said on the inspection ground. Two stood empty beside the barrel stacks.

Wheelwrights lifted each suspect axle onto trestles, withdrew the retaining pins and removed the wheels. They scraped the journals, renewed the grease, fitted replacement wedges and checked the track of the repaired carts under a load. The smiths straightened bent shoes while the stores clerks weighed sacks against the company lists. Drill continued, shortened on the days assigned to loading and repairs.

Arra’s first new field chest reached the Moat on the eighteenth. It paid the preparation charges the companies had carried while the money travelled. The four outstanding scouts returned in two pairs, with accounts and reports rather than a complete picture of the south. Most of the men excused drill recovered; twenty remained unfit for marching at the final inspection and were assigned care at the Moat.

Jacaerys had left at dawn on the sixth. He returned on the sixteenth with Rhaenyra’s answer.

The queen had sealed the military articles without putting Jon under Daemon: northern command, consent to detachments, and up to two years of southern service, ending sooner with the war. The port and merchant-fleet permissions were granted. The future customs schedule stood as written. The two city-chartering rights and her backing for the separate commercial negotiations were included.

A separate writ recognized Stark hereditary lordship over both Gifts under her claimed royal authority, reserving six tenths of the defined land revenues for the Watch and pledging her support in negotiating the settlement.

“It names both grants,” Jacaerys said. “The writ is hers. Possession will still have to be settled with the brothers. She has written to them as well.”

There was no substitute six-moon service clause and no demand that Jon abandon his own command. The copies were checked against the endorsed articles. Jon’s already offered service could proceed. Arra received the royal papers later; the writ did not displace the brothers’ reeves or cancel the agreement she had signed.

By the evening of the nineteenth the first preparation was complete. The army had 280 serviceable wagons, its animals inspected and its loads divided. The storekeeper counted 1,092 quarters of grain. Seven hundred and twenty were assigned to the moving army; the rest stayed at the Moat for the depot and following deliveries. On the twentieth, 7,080 northerners began moving south. A hundred and twenty remained on depot, repair and medical duties, including the twenty men still unfit.

### The road to the Trident

The host cleared the southern end of the Neck on the eleventh day of the fifth moon. Infantry companies used the road in succession; wagon blocks followed their assigned escorts. Road parties cut drainage, replaced bad decking and made passing places where a stalled cart would otherwise stop everything behind it. They did not attempt to pave the kingsroad.

A Frey officer met the advance with a letter bearing Forrest’s seal and a second instruction from Sabitha. Their bridge would remain under Frey guard. Northern messengers could use it; purchases and crossings would be entered by their officers. They would provide guides for the western roads, but their stores were not offered without payment.

At the first agreed supply station, the northern quartermaster and the Frey factor counted the sacks together.

“The lord’s men have already taken the best oats,” the factor said. “There is barley. Your horses will eat it.”

The quartermaster tested a measure from the lower sacks before accepting the consignment. The contract named the next delivery as well as the present one. A Frey messenger remained with the advance so that patrols on the two sides could recognize each other’s orders.

Mallister agreed to watch the Seagard approaches and exchange coastal reports. Vypren and Charlton supplied local guides and warnings through the Frey connection. Their soldiers stayed chiefly in their own holdings. Northern troops were not posted in every castle to make the agreements appear larger.

Riverrun gave a narrower answer. Elmo permitted correspondence and purchases through willing suppliers, but would not bring the Tully banners out against his grandfather’s position. Grover still favoured Aegon. The riverlords cooperating with Jon did not thereby become Stark bannermen, and Riverrun was not entered as a northern headquarters.

At the Trident, Roote’s officers allotted ferry turns, landing space and a place for the wagons to wait. Men unloaded the heaviest carts. Sacks went onto the ferry separately, with their tally tags tied beneath the lashings. Draught teams were taken across in smaller groups. Darry guides checked the road beyond while the rear companies came up. The crossing occupied several days; supply purchases continued on both banks.

News reached the column in pieces. Cole had replaced Otto as Hand. The Cargyll brothers had died during an attempt on Rhaenyra at Dragonstone. Duskendale had fallen. Later came the report from Rook’s Rest: Rhaenys and Meleys dead, Aegon and Sunfyre badly hurt, Aemond exercising the regency.

Jacaerys’s account said that his mother had forbidden her sons to join the relief. Rhaenys had gone alone and been met by Sunfyre and Vhagar. The new sea lookouts had brought no warning of the trap behind Cole’s army. Jon’s nearest companies had still been on the northern roads.

### Harrenhal and the cooperating lords

Jon’s leading companies reached Harrenhal on the sixteenth day of the sixth moon. Daemon’s liaison had met the column earlier and brought the arrangements for stores, camp ground and the roads to the castle.

“The eastward yards,” Daemon said when they met. “Your quartermaster has been shown them. He is not to count my stores among his own.”

“He has purchase agreements,” Jon said. “I need your officers to recognize the carriers using them.”

Daemon called for the officer keeping the gate lists. They settled the means of identifying convoys and the reports to be shared with Roote and Darry. The northern companies kept their command. Daemon retained Harrenhal and his own following. Neither surrendered his force to the other.

Blackwood’s household joined the arrangements through its representatives. The surrender at Stone Hedge was confirmed from Daemon’s record, not fought again. Jon pressed for protection of compliant Bracken tenants and for complaints to be heard against named offenders. One cattle claim was settled by returning animals; another remained disputed when the Blackwood officer next left the council. No general permission to burn Bracken farms was issued.

Atranta would not declare for Rhaenyra. Its refusal was entered separately from the cooperation of the Vances of Wayfarer’s Rest. Jon’s couriers kept the approach open, but the main army was not sent west to besiege a castle that did not bar its supply road.

Reports from the Crownlands still placed the Greens’ principal strength around the captured coastal castles and the capital. Daemon kept Caraxes near Harrenhal between his own flights. The northern screen watched the eastern approaches; it was not sent to provoke Vhagar into a battle.

By the seventh moon, carts had made repeated journeys between the forward stations and Harrenhal. Roote and Darry guarded their own approaches. Frey kept the western crossing and the upper-road relays; Mallister covered the northwest. Small northern detachments protected the army’s stores and supported the local guards. The main force remained concentrated near Harrenhal.

The road was not free of fighting. On the twenty-eighth day of the sixth moon, mounted men carrying Green colours struck a convoy on the Darry road. The escort drew the surviving carts into a farm enclosure and held its gate. Ryswell’s mounted patrol arrived with local men before the attackers could remove the remaining loads. They drove the raiders off and took prisoners, but two wagons had burned and six northerners were dead. Twenty-seven more needed treatment.

The prisoners were recorded and delivered under the agreed local jurisdiction. Captured men who submitted were not killed to pay for the dead. The convoy resumed with replacement carts; the next one had a larger escort through that stretch.

### The North during the advance

Arra’s letters followed the supply route and, once available, the cooperating castles’ ravens. She continued the Watch work without returning its decisions to Jon.

The Lord Commander protested Rhaenyra’s restoration writ and did not surrender either Gift. Arra preserved the six-village agreement while the broader title remained contested. Her surveyors checked the holdings; by the latest report, twenty-four willing households had moved into repaired dwellings in three of the villages. The other three had been surveyed but not resettled. She had spent 14,800 dragons of her reserved allowance. No new harvest or substantial rent had yet been collected.

At Bear Island the promised timber wharf came into use. The stone enclosure was still under construction. Reed had one raised store and one landing operating, four boats delivered and the initial food distributed or put under cover. Their remaining work continued under the original gifts. Neither place had become the completed port described in the royal articles.

Winter Town’s charter had been read in its hall. Dustin supplied his consent for Barrowton’s charter. The instruments gave the towns their legal privileges; the inhabitants still needed roofs, customers and work. White Harbor began work on its own dock and shipyard plans with Manderly’s money. The royal customs concession had not begun: the agreed programme was not complete.

Dragonstone issued the promised commercial protections. Corlys and Manderly agreed reciprocal access for resident factors and warehouse leases while retaining their own port dues. No free northern cargoes were promised away. The larger merchant fleet remained a building and financing undertaking, not something produced by the seals.

Jacaerys’s later correspondence described fast vessels stationed farther out, arrangements for passing their warnings ashore, and food distributions at Driftmark and Dragonstone. The relief did not open the capital’s markets or feed everyone affected by the blockade. During the eighth moon he reported four new dragonriders after the dangerous claiming attempts on Dragonstone. Their training and coordination were still work in progress.

### The nineteenth day of the eighth moon

Of the original 7,200, twenty-three had died: six in the convoy fighting, fourteen from sickness and three in accidents. Forty-nine had been sent north unfit for continued service. The active expedition numbered 7,128. Harrenhal held 6,800 of them, including seventy temporarily unfit; 280 served at the supply stations and forty-eight were scouting or carrying messages. No replacement companies had arrived from the North.

The army’s own stores held 1,916 quarters across the four stations, with 1,020 at Harrenhal. Its field accounts showed 62,622 dragons still in the chest. Food, service, animals, transport and repairs had all been paid from recorded transfers and purchases, not from the value of promised charters.

At the evening council Roote’s officer asked when a borrowed team would be returned. Harrenhal’s storekeeper wanted the following week’s barley unloaded before another company used the yard for drill. The quartermaster put the delivery dates beside the guard roster while the lords considered the roads beyond the area they were already keeping supplied.

### Changed this turn

The fourteen-day preparation and the following four moons are complete. Rhaenyra ratified the agreed military and royal articles; the host departed the Moat on 129/04/20 and cleared the Neck on 129/05/11. The two-year maximum service runs from that crossing, ending sooner if the war ends. Jon reached Harrenhal on 129/06/16 and retains independent northern command.

A working supply and intelligence corridor now links the Moat, the upper Riverlands, the Trident and Harrenhal. Frey, Mallister, Vypren, Charlton, Roote, Darry and Blackwood cooperate on the recorded terms. Riverrun allows limited dealings but has not joined the war; Atranta remains outside the arrangement. Allied land has not become Stark property, and Stone Hedge was not conquered a second time.

The expedition has 7,128 active men after twenty-three deaths and forty-nine medical returns north. Of these, 6,730 are fit at Harrenhal, seventy unfit there, 280 at depots and forty-eight away on duty. Expedition grain is 1,916 quarters; the field chest is 62,622 GD. Detailed payments, transfers, the dated Winterfell account and outstanding building commitments are in the records.

The royal Gift writ exists, but the Watch contests the transfer and retains possession beyond Arra’s implemented six-village agreement. Her work remains delegated. City and commercial instruments have advanced; neither the complete naval programme nor its future customs clock is finished. Rook’s Rest and the subsequent dragon-claiming attempts have changed the southern war while the North established its corridor.
