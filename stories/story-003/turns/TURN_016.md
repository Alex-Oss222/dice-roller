## Turn 16 | 129/04/05 | Evening
Elapsed: Twenty minutes.

Jon took the prince away from the command tent to the trees beside the fallen wall. Men had begun calling the patch a godswood, though there was no tended enclosure. Two guards remained by the gap in the stones. Ice was brought from Jon's baggage in its wool wrapping.

He laid the greatsword across a folded hide, wetted a fine stone and passed it lightly along the edge. He worked from the base towards the point, turned the blade, and repeated the passes on the other side. The edge had no notch to grind out.

“There is nothing sharper than Valyrian steel. It needs no whetstone, but it is always better to be prepared.”

Jacaerys stood on the other side of the fallen masonry, his mother's letter still with him.

“Tell me, Black Prince, what can House Stark do for you? It has been over a hundred years since Aegon's conquest. The last time Targaryens came north, they took food from my people by granting the Gift to the Night's Watch. What can your mother offer me to send good northern men south to die in a Targaryen quarrel? Your house has done nothing for the North. Let me hear her terms from her son's lips.”

Beyond the trees, a supply wagon turned off the kingsroad that Jaehaerys had extended north to Winterfell and the Wall. Its driver called for someone to move a stack of hurdles.

“Queen Alysanne paid for a castle at the Wall as well as giving it land,” Jacaerys said. “The brothers there defend northerners. I will not agree that she did nothing for them.”

He put the letter on a flat stone, clear of the wet hide.

“But you asked what my mother offers. Confirmation of your lordship, your wardenship and the North's customary rights. Your men remain under you, or the captain you appoint. You would have a place in her war council while your host serves. And I will offer a year's remission of the Crown's levy upon Winterfell after she has secured the throne.”

He continued before Jon had answered.

“The payment Winterfell sends the Crown, not every harbour due or a debt owed to another house. I would set that before her for her seal. I have brought no treasury with me.”

“She asks you to declare for her and bring a northern host to join Prince Daemon at Harrenhal. You would command your people. He must direct the armies together. She needs your service until her throne is secured; I have not brought a date on which the men may all go home.”

Jacaerys opened the outer fold of the letter so that its seal and address lay clear.

“My grandsire named her heir while he lived. Your house swore to uphold that succession. She does not consider this only a quarrel between her brothers and herself.”

“My brother went to Storm's End with a message. The word sent to me is that Aemond pursued him on Vhagar and killed him. Lucerys is dead. That is what has followed my mother's refusal to surrender.”

“As for the Gift, I have no authority to surrender the Watch's lands. I can carry a demand to my mother. I can negotiate the service you give her. I cannot put her seal on a new grant myself.” He left the unfolded letter within Jon's reach. “Those are my opening terms, Lord Stark. Tell me what you require beyond them.”
