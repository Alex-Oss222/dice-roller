## Turn 17 | 129/04/05 | Evening
Elapsed: Forty minutes.

“Confirm my lordship?” Jon said. “I took the North back from my uncle. Your mother did not put me there. Unless I break the king’s peace, I do not need her leave to govern what generations of Starks have governed. She failed to see the Greens coming, and you offer me what I already hold.”

“Then do not count it as payment,” Jacaerys said. “I do not dispute your inheritance. I came asking for your army, not bringing back your uncle.”

Jon set out the command terms first. Every northern soldier would remain under a commander appointed by Winterfell. No company could be detached without his consent. The campaign’s objectives and duration must be agreed before the host marched. Those conditions were not for bargaining away.

“And I want both Gifts in Stark hands, by your mother’s own writ. Six tenths of the proceeds for the Watch, four for Winterfell. The Watch will not decide whether she may grant it. We will settle the rest here. Reach an agreement, and I will ride south with my men.”

The camp clerk brought Jon’s prepared brief and a writing board. He pinned the corners beneath two wooden slips, ruled a margin and copied the command article separately. The prince waited while the clerk read it back.

“I accept your commander and your consent to detachments,” Jacaerys said. “For the campaign, I propose six moons from the day your host crosses the Neck. Join Daemon at Harrenhal and fight the armies opposing us in the riverlands. He coordinates the allied hosts; he does not divide yours. A different theatre or service beyond those six moons requires another agreement with you.”

The clerk entered the proposed term beneath Jon’s article, leaving space for an answer.

They worked through the other demands. Jon distinguished White Harbor’s shipyards, repair slips, warehouses, naval stores and defensive ships from Bear Island’s fortified landing, patrol craft, provisions and repair yard. He included experienced southern shipwrights and naval officers to train northerners, with Manderly participating in the eastern works.

“I will not promise two harbours’ worth of building without a price,” Jacaerys said. “Instead of the year’s remission I offered, I will recommend that you retain half Winterfell’s royal levy for three years, for agreed naval works. The money stays north. The account names what is built. That requires my mother’s seal.”

He asked the clerk to specify that the arrangement would begin with the first payment falling due after ratification, not depend upon the capture of King’s Landing.

“Permission to engage willing craftsmen and officers can go into the same articles. Men already serving aboard our ships need release from their service. I cannot promise their captains will spare everyone you choose.”

Jon required separate commercial instruments: royal privileges, including at Dragonstone, and Corlys’s agreement for Driftmark, Hull and Spicetown. Reduced charges, resident factors, warehouse and market access, and stated procedures for disputes would have reciprocal northern counterparts. Timber, wool, hides and provisions were trade, not free gifts to the queen’s fleet.

“Reciprocal terms I will support,” the prince said. “The actual rates must be written. My grandfather must answer for his ports, and Manderly for what you offer at his.”

For the cities, Jon named Winter Town and, with Dustin’s agreement, Barrowton. The grant was to be a perpetual right to charter two cities at approved northern sites, with the markets, fairs, associations, courts, warehouses, fortifications and specified dues written into it. A surveyed Bay of Ice harbour could replace the second site; it was not a demand for a third.

“I will support those two charters before the queen. Have the landholding lords’ consent put with them. I will not promise Dustin’s rights away while asking you to trust us with yours.”

“The ink is dry,” the clerk said.

When Jacaerys lifted the draft, a line transferred onto the sheet beneath it. He laid the two sheets apart and returned to the remaining article.

“Brandon’s Gift was given by a Stark. The Watch holds that grant as well as Alysanne’s. Ordering both taken from them is not confirmation of land you already possess.”

He read the proposed division of proceeds again.

“I will carry the demand for both, with the six tenths you offer. I will not pretend my mother has granted it, or promise that the Watch’s claim disappears because she writes an order. She must decide whether she will undertake that dispute for your support.”

The prince asked for fair copies but did not call for seals. “Is possession of both Gifts a condition of your marching, Lord Stark? I need to know what answer I am carrying to my mother.”

### Changed this turn

Jon has presented his full counterterms to Jacaerys. The prince accepts the northern command safeguards in principle and proposes a six-moon Riverlands campaign beginning when the host crosses the Neck, with any extension or different theatre requiring a new agreement.

Jacaerys will recommend the two city-chartering rights and reciprocal commercial protections, and proposes three years’ retention of half Winterfell’s royal levy for priced naval works instead of his opening one-year remission. Royal ratification, local agreements, exact commercial rates and Jon’s acceptance remain outstanding. No financial concession is in force.

Jacaerys will convey the demand for both Gifts but will not promise royal confiscation or treat the Watch’s title as extinguished. Arra’s signed settlement and delegated work remain in force. No pledge, dispatch or deployment has been made.
