## Turn 19 | 129/04/05 | Night
Elapsed: One hour and fifteen minutes.

“It has been a pleasure, Black Prince. I will have copies made and sent to Winterfell. We march for a queen when the men are ready, no later than three weeks from now. I wish you luck in the battles to come.”

Jacaerys had gathered his papers, but Jon kept him a little longer.

“I hear your good-father holds the Gullet. That is power, but it is also a weight. The Triarchy still remembers the Stepstones. They know how to fight ships and dragons both. Take care that the fleet is not caught at anchor or with its eyes only on the capital. Keep fast ships and lookouts farther out, and see that the dragonriders are ready to answer as one, not one by one. A single dragon can burn a ship; five working together can break a fleet before it ever closes.”

“My grandfather will hear it from me,” Jacaerys said. “I will ask him about the outer watch and how warning reaches the riders. They cannot answer together if half of them hear it late.”

“And while the blockade stands, do not forget the smallfolk on both sides of the water. Kings and lords wage their wars, but it is the common people who bear the brunt of empty markets and empty bellies. If the Velaryon ships can spare a portion of what they take, or Dragonstone and Driftmark can feed those suffering most from the closing of the sea, the blockade will cost the Greens more in loyalty than it costs you in grain.”

“On Driftmark we can have our own men distribute food,” the prince said. “Across the water, we would need someone to receive it who will not sell it to a garrison. I will raise that with my mother as well. I cannot promise my grandfather's captains or his stores before speaking to him.”

He asked the clerk to put the naval warning and the relief proposal on a separate leaf with the articles. His departure remained set for dawn.

“Three weeks,” Jacaerys said. “Then I know when you need her answer. I shall put that before her first.”

The clerk copied the endorsed articles for Winterfell, checked the numbered clauses against Jon's copy and recorded that he had examined the prince's signature and seal. He folded the transcript inside a covering letter, passed a cord through the wrapper and closed it under Stark wax. It went north that night with a supply-road courier. The covering letter named Rhaenyra as Jon's chosen cause, stated that her ratification was still awaited, and directed Arra to circulate the same notice to the northern houses. Her authority and the Watch agreement were left undisturbed.

A runner went through the company streets summoning the lords and the senior captains of those whose lords were absent. Jon returned to the command tent. Cerwyn and Roderick Dustin arrived first; Tallhart, Ryswell, Hornwood and Glover followed. The Manderly and Dreadfort companies sent their officers.

“There is room for everyone,” the clerk said. The last two captains could not get inside and listened from the entrance.

“We prepare to march for Rhaenyra,” Jon told them. “As soon as the men are ready. No later than the twenty-sixth.”

Dustin asked whether the queen had sealed the bargain.

“Not yet. Her son carries it back tomorrow.”

The command article was read aloud. Winterfell would appoint the northern commander; no southern captain could detach a company without Jon's agreement. The proposed service ran for at most two years after leaving the Neck, ending sooner if the war ended. Those were the terms going to the queen, not orders received from Daemon.

The captains were told to bring their marching returns in the morning: men fit for the road, wagons, draught animals, provisions and outstanding pay. The camp storekeeper separated grain already under cover from deliveries still expected. The clerk left the absent scouts on their own line. The eighteen men excused drill remained on the treatment return.

Dustin stayed beside the route sheet while the captains sorted the returns by company. “Where do you mean to take us once we are through the Neck? I need to tell my wagon master how far he is provisioning for.”
