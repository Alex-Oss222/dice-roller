## Turn 21 | 130/01/01 | Night
Elapsed: 132 days, from 129/08/19. The Gullet campaign has not yet been fought.

### Harrenhal: the two fronts

Daemon received Jon with the western road reports spread beside the correspondence from Dragonstone. He had not summoned every riverlord to Harrenhal. Some of the seals belonged to men whose troops were still guarding their own crossings.

“The westermen first,” Jon said. “I will meet them before they can join Cole. Keep the eastern roads watched. When the western army is dealt with, I come back against Cole. We must keep his men and Vhagar from fighting the same battle.”

“And if my nephew brings his dragon to your battle?”

“Then we need warning before he arrives. Not a raven afterward telling us how bravely we died.”

Daemon agreed to hold the eastern approaches through the cooperating riverlords, without committing them to defend an exposed camp beneath Vhagar. He would ask the queen for another rider. Jon's reconnaissance orders went west through Ryswell, with eighty suitable men drawn from the existing Bolton company. Roote and Darry kept separate reports on Cole's marching columns and sightings of Aemond. The same sighting copied into three letters was entered once.

The first joint returns accounted for 8,400 rivermen available for agreed field service across the region. They included men already cooperating with Daemon. They were spread from Seagard to the Trident. The clerk struck two companies from a later return when their captains' names appeared on Daemon's first list.

Jon's reinforcement order went north through Arra. The returned allotments provided 4,600 additional land troops in successive bodies, with 120 men assigned to two Manderly coasters. Together with the original 7,200, these orders called 11,920 northerners in all. Each contributing house retained its required home strength. The new troops still had to assemble and march.

Letters went separately to Jeyne Arryn and to the queen about Vale troops and their protection. Corlys received the requests for better maritime reporting, inspected relief passages and precautions for royal passengers. There was no order sending northern infantry after a wounded dragon at Rook's Rest.

On the ninth day of the ninth moon, the household clerk entered Jon's twenty-second nameday. The western couriers arrived during the same week. They had seen Lannister wagons, remount herds and companies moving out of the hills. Piper and Vance asked for assistance at the Red Fork.

Jon's host left Harrenhal on the fourteenth. Daemon remained in the east. Nettles reached him on Sheepstealer three days later, under the queen's orders for joint reconnaissance and operations. Hugh and Ulf were not sent away together with independent authority over a province. Their conduct remained something to observe, not a conviction for a betrayal they had not committed.

### The Red Fork: sixteenth day of the tenth moon

Jon reached the threatened crossings with 6,500 northerners. The participating riverlords brought 6,800 men, leaving the eastern covering forces and their own necessary garrisons in place. The Lannister army opposite was reported at between eleven and fourteen thousand. Its captured muster would later account for 12,400 men before the crossing fights.

Lord Jason did not throw his entire army at one defended ford. His men demonstrated downstream while Adrian Tarbeck took boats to a mill landing farther up the river. Their first crossing drove back the local watch. By the time Ryswell's messenger found Jon, westermen had occupied the mill buildings and were bringing horses across.

Jon sent the nearer reserve towards the landing. He kept Dustin's principal division at the main ford. Pulling it away would have given the Lannisters another crossing while the boats were being contested.

At the mill lane, Piper's men were giving ground between a ditch and a broken orchard wall. Jon dismounted behind the arriving Stark spears and had Ice brought forward. His standard stayed on the rise with the officer repeating his orders.

“Close the lane. Two ranks through that opening. The rest hold until the archers are past you.”

The first northern company met the westermen before it had finished dressing its line. A horse pushed into the spears and fell across the ditch. Men climbed over it. Others tried the orchard wall. Jon took the next rank into the gap rather than send it around a flank he could not see.

A mailed man came over the wall with an axe. Jon caught the descending haft against Ice's guard, forced it aside and drove the point beneath the man's raised arm. He withdrew before the next spearman reached him. That man's thrust struck Jon's breastplate and slid across it. Jon stepped outside the shaft, cut at the exposed neck above the collar and let the following northern rank take the ground.

There was no room for broad swings. He used the greatsword's reach down the lane, then shortened his grip as the men pressed closer. A spearhead caught his left forearm at the edge of the gauntlet. The mail checked it, but the cut underneath bled into his sleeve.

“Keep the rank,” he told the men coming after him. “Do not follow them into the boats.”

Blackwood archers had reached the higher orchard ground. Their arrows struck the boat crews and the men waiting to land. The northern reserve stopped the bridgehead from widening; it did not make the westermen disappear. They fought from the mill doors and behind the stacked grain until their officers could no longer bring another company across.

Jason had crossed to reinforce the landing before its approaches closed. His horse was killed during the withdrawal. A river knight identified him among the wounded prisoners. Adrian Tarbeck escaped in one of the last boats.

At the principal ford, Dustin held through two sustained attacks without pursuing into the river. Once the mill landing was lost, Lefford and the surviving western captains withdrew the army's uncommitted formations. Northern horse followed far enough to confirm the retreat and take abandoned carts, then returned to support. No march on Casterly Rock followed.

The western army had lost its crossing, much of its forward baggage and its lord. It had not been killed to the last man. Over the following week the prisoner and burial returns counted 1,180 western dead and 2,760 captives; other losses remained uncertain among the men retreating west. Jason survived his wounds in custody.

Northern losses, including men who subsequently died of their wounds, reached 180 dead. Another 585 wounded survived. The riverlords' companies had suffered heavily at the first landing and the ford, losing 340 men dead after their wounded were accounted for. Their stores and local families took in men the army could not immediately move.

At the dressing station, a surgeon cut away Jon's sleeve, washed the wound, removed two broken links and closed the cut with three stitches. He laid folded linen over it and bound the forearm. Jon continued issuing orders, but did not resume weapons practice until the wound had closed. By the next moon he could use the arm normally; the scar remained.

### While Jon was west

Daemon's messages reached the western camp through the agreed relays. Cole had begun his northern advance on the tenth day of the tenth moon. Aemond accompanied the operation, but did not remain continuously above its marching columns.

The eastern riverlords emptied the exposed military stores, moved threatened households away from the immediate road and guarded the crossings they could defend. They did not burn the whole countryside. Cole found defended passages, evacuated depots and hostile scouts rather than a northern army waiting outside Harrenhal for him to burn.

Daemon and Nettles flew together when pursuing a confirmed sighting. Aemond attacked several settlements beyond their immediate reach. Some warnings arrived early enough to move people; others did not. Caraxes could not guard every roof between the Trident and the Blackwater.

Then the news from the capital changed Cole's position.

Rhaenyra entered King's Landing on the eighth day of the eleventh moon. Her own ships and dragons had moved after reports confirmed the eastern army's departure. Men of the City Watch admitted her supporters. Daemon's contacts had helped arrange that opening, but Daemon himself was still in the riverlands, not simultaneously leading the landing.

The subsequent letters named Alicent among the prisoners and reported the executions of Otto Hightower and Jasper Wylde. Aegon had escaped with help from within the court. His whereabouts were not in the letters. The treasury recovered in the capital was much smaller than the queen had expected.

Jon returned to Harrenhal on the tenth. Cole was now north of a capital held by his enemies, with the western army retreating and the road to the Reach offering his best prospect of joining another Green host. The reports of his movements and those of Vhagar began to separate.

The first two thousand northern reinforcements reached Harrenhal on the twentieth, after their own long march. They relieved depot and castle duties and supplied a following reserve. They had not fought at the Red Fork. The other 2,600 were still coming down the established route.

### Cole's retreat: twenty-ninth day of the eleventh moon

Cole moved around the western shore of the Gods Eye towards the southern roads. Jon followed with the experienced part of his host, joined by riverlord companies familiar with the crossings. Local mounted parties watched the alternative routes. They took supply carts when they could and directed civilians away from the fighting, rather than poisoning wells their own neighbours would need afterward.

Near the southern roads, Jon placed the main blocking force beyond a narrow crossing and kept a second body behind a low ridge on Cole's flank. The Green column could not use the crossing and remain in marching order under the archers. It deployed instead.

Cole had about 4,600 men with him. Jon's fighting force was 6,350 northerners and 4,300 rivermen. The newly arrived northern companies were securing the corridor and the following baggage. The Valemen had not yet reached Harrenhal.

Cole struck before every allied company had reached its place. His mounted men drove at the thinner riverlord wing while infantry pressed the crossing. The first charge broke through a line of archers and reached the wagons behind them. Jon sent his reserve towards the breach, not towards the white cloak he could see farther down the field.

He rode with the reserve until the ground narrowed around the carts, then dismounted. The nearest captain had fallen. Jon took his place long enough to turn two companies towards the flank of the intruding horsemen.

A rider leaned out of his saddle to strike at him. Jon moved behind the cart shaft, let the sword hit the timber and thrust Ice up under the rider's arm. Another horseman came through before he could recover the blade. A northern spearman intercepted him; the horse carried both men into the wheel. Jon pulled Ice clear and called the following rank through the space they had opened.

The reserve's arrival stopped the break from spreading. It did not end the fighting at the crossing, where Cole's infantry were still advancing under shields.

Aemond arrived while those companies were engaged.

Vhagar came above the western trees. Jon's messengers carried the dispersal order to the companies not yet committed. Men left the wagon lanes and moved into the folds of the ground. Those already fighting could not simply disappear. Dragonfire struck the outer baggage ground, and teams broke their traces among the carts.

Caraxes climbed from the northern side of the field. Sheepstealer came farther east. Jon could see the dragons separate, turn and close, but not what their riders were saying or which wounds the first collision caused. Vhagar turned away from another pass over the ground troops to meet them.

The fighting below continued. With no second sweep of dragonfire behind his assault, Cole could not turn the broken baggage ground into a general rout. The blocking companies held. The force behind the ridge came forward against his exposed flank, and his infantry began withdrawing towards their own wagons.

Jon kept a passage open for men laying down their weapons. Sergeants took their shields and spearheads before allowing them through. One prisoner said he had no other knife. A guard pulled another from inside his boot, put it with the collected weapons and sent him on to the enclosure.

Cole's guard held longer than the companies around it. He was taken alive after his horse went down and the surviving men beside him were surrounded. The surviving guards surrendered with him. His captors removed his sword and morningstar and brought him under escort to Jon's officers.

By the final count, 2,610 Greens were prisoners and 690 had been found dead. Others had escaped or scattered from the field. The force had ceased to function as Cole's army; not every man who fled was entered as killed.

The northern return eventually recorded 130 dead from the battle and subsequent wounds. Among the surviving wounded, some resumed duty, some remained under treatment and others were removed from active service. The prisoners received food from a separate issue, guarded outside the companies' own sleeping ground.

### The dragon fight

Daemon returned the next day. Caraxes landed away from the occupied yards and kept one wing partly folded. Nettles came later with Sheepstealer; her mount had burns and tears along a wing edge. She had a burned leg and required treatment.

Their accounts supplied what Jon had not been able to see from below. Daemon had turned Vhagar away from the ground attack while Nettles came against her from a different direction. Aemond tried to close with Caraxes. Sheepstealer's attack prevented him keeping the smaller dragon trapped against Vhagar's weight.

They had injured Vhagar, but both Black dragons had also been hurt. Daemon broke off rather than pursue after Nettles lost formation. Aemond flew southwest. No one had recovered his body or seen Vhagar fall.

“He is alive until someone proves otherwise,” Daemon said. “Do not send your wagons out on the strength of what the men are singing.”

For the following weeks the two Black dragons needed rest and care. They could no longer supply the same continuous cover. The queen received that report as well as the news of Cole's capture. A wounded Vhagar remained a threat; there had been no fatal duel above Harrenhal and no reason to count one in advance.

### The other forces and the turn of the year

Jeyne Arryn agreed to send three thousand men, not fifteen thousand. The queen provided an actual period of Seasmoke's protection for the Gulltown embarkations, and the ships made two crossings to Maidenpool. Leowyn Corbray brought the contingent onward under its own officers. It reached the allied concentration during the twelfth moon, after the battle with Cole.

Further Riverland returns brought the total committed field companies to eleven thousand over the campaign. This included the original 8,400, not another eleven thousand on top of them. Their men were divided between the western frontier, eastern roads, depots and the field army. Riverrun still had not sent its banners. Atranta had not submitted. The westermen who escaped were being gathered behind their own approaches, where reports of ironborn raiding gave their lords another demand to meet.

The final northern body arrived on the twenty-eighth day of the twelfth moon. All 4,600 newly called land troops had now reached the expedition. Manderly's two coasters, with 120 men, had separately reached Corlys's service for the agreed reconnaissance and liaison work. Their captains now sent their reports through the Velaryon shore stations.

At sea, the outer watch and shore signals continued. Corlys expanded measured relief where stores and distributors were available. Jacaerys's report said that the queen's younger sons had been brought to King's Landing under escort after the capital's capture. They had not sailed unprotected towards Pentos. Reports of Triarchy preparations still required watching; the loss of Cole's army did not make the Narrow Sea safe.

Arra kept governing while Jon fought. Her expenditure on the six villages reached 21,600 dragons. Thirty-nine willing households were now settled in four of them; the other two had repairs and survey work but no settled households. The Watch still contested the wider royal writ. She continued the limited agreement without asking Jon to approve another round of clauses.

Mormont's curtain remained unfinished. Reed's second store, landing and the remaining boats came into use, with final account work outstanding. The complete port and merchant-fleet programme was still not finished, and the royal customs clock had not begun. Brandon's wardship term had ended; Arra's latest packet reminded Jon of the different answer he had promised him. She had not supplied that answer in Jon's name.

At Harrenhal on the first night of 130, the clerks closed the campaign return. Of the northerners called since the first summons, 381 were dead and 393 had been removed from active service, alive. Some of those men were home; others were still in hospitals or travelling north. The active land expedition stood at 11,026, with another 120 serving aboard the two ships. Jon's forearm had healed with a scar.

A royal messenger brought a request that Criston Cole be sent to King's Landing. The prisoner remained under northern guard. Jon's quartermaster brought the next convoy schedule to the same table. The clerk set out a fresh sheet for the reply while the guard captain waited to learn whether his prisoner was travelling.
