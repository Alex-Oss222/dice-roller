### The great grain reserve

The **ten buildings add places to keep 10,000 quarters**, which is not the same thing as adding 10,000 quarters to the harvest. Raised floors, wide eaves, sound roofing, ventilation, a lockable dry store, separated sacks and protection against vermin are the business of the work. There must be a clear way for carts to come and go and a keeper who can account for each issue.

The stores officer must first measure the existing sound grain space, identify damaged sacks or grain and show how much overflow there has been over the last year. A storehouse that would merely stand empty should remain unbuilt. The first **two** granaries may be tendered when the shortages and sites are proved; the other eight should follow measured need, not the attractive round number of ten. Their construction estimates are taken from the price book; extraordinary building on unsuitable foundations is not included.

The grain book should show, for each kind of grain: the opening quantity, fresh receipts, seed issued, men and animals provisioned, grain sold or sent away, wastage and the closing quantity. None of the Watch's existing grain gift is to be counted a second time as a cost of these buildings.

### The works room and standards

A small account room needs a sound door, a chest for sealed contracts, copies of the receiver's warrants, a chest or rack for surviving tallies, and a clerk to compare payment with completion. **The 12-dragon entry is solely a supplies allowance**. It does not erect a new office building or hire a new council.

The master measures are checked against one another by a smith and a trusted merchant who understands weights. **Thirty-five sets are intended for the chief seats of Stark's thirty-five direct bannermen, and one for Winter Town.** They do not automatically override the rights of those lords or their subordinate houses. Each set is issued with a receipt stating its custody and whose seal certifies it. The 200 dragons for the whole work is a provisional price; if iron, graduated vessels and honest scales cost more, the steward must bring a separate quotation.

No fee for using these weights may be taken without a lawful right and an announced rate.

### Clerks' school

The **clerks' school** is one modest house in the service precinct, where a clerk-teacher at book pay (6.38 a year) teaches about twenty sons of receivers, reeves and tally clerks to reckon, tally and keep the grain book, so the reports and audits already in place do not lapse as the present clerks age. It runs at about **25 dragons a year** (teacher, twenty pupils' keep at 0.70 each, materials), met from the household account. It creates no office or council and promises no pupil a post.

### Wells and baths near the castle

The **three wells** must be spaced where they actually serve distinct castle tasks, such as a grain-working yard, a busy service court and an area otherwise badly supplied. A survey may move a proposed well or cancel one. There is no authority to declare the three wells already dug. Each must be tested for yield and covered safely.

The **two castle bathhouses** are separate buildings from all five proposed at Winter Town. Each **225-dragon estimate** consists of a **roughly 85.22-dragon stone-and-timber house-price comparison** plus **139.78 dragons of assumed tubs, heating vessels, fire protection, floor, flues, drain and fittings**. This is **not** an existing bathhouse price in the book. Winterfell has hot springs in the setting, but no one should promise a new bath can be fed by a convenient existing pipe without inspecting the actual route and castle arrangements. Otherwise it needs vessels and fuel to heat water.

The baths are for household use unless Jon gives different instructions. They do not earn a new rent merely by being completed. A keeper must be assigned within lawful household service, and the cost of fuel, washing and repairs must enter the ordinary household account.

### Paved approach and service yards

The **approach and gate court** (about 2,900 square yards: a quarter-mile, 20 feet wide) and the **service yards** (about 29,000 square yards, assumed to be six acres) are paved in rubble cobbles bedded in sand on a gravel base. The price book has a gravel road at 203.72 a mile but **no paving line**, so these are **allowances awaiting a paviour's quotation**. The rate is the book's road bill of quantities plus a two-inch sand bed and a five-inch course of building rubble at 0.2272 per long ton, laid at an assumed 12 square yards per paviour-day, with the book's 15% allowance: **about 744 dragons per mile of 12-foot street, or 105.70 per 1,000 square yards**. The areas are unsurveyed, and the charge moves in proportion to them. Repairs already ordered to these surfaces are deducted, not charged twice.

All paving in this roll, castle and town together, moves **about 29,800 long tons** of stone, gravel and sand, roughly **19,900 wagon loads** at an assumed 1½ tons. Wagons, teams and paviours, not coin, set the pace. The approach, market square and main street come first (about 26 loads a day through one working season of about 140 days, assumed to fall between the fourth and ninth moons); the side lanes and yards follow in 127 and 128 (about 58 loads a day) once the first paving has been inspected after a thaw.

Stock stone while frozen ground carries wagons, and lay nothing on frozen ground. Bed it in sand, not lime mortar, so the surface can move with frost. Crown each street and lead its gutters to a ditch clear of every well, bath and the tannery. Survey buried drains and any hot-water runs under the yards before a yard is lifted. No quarry or gravel pit is yet identified; stone from a bannerman's land is bought at the ordinary price, and no lord's consent is needed to pave Stark's own ground.

### Who is to be heard, and when

Jon speaks first to **the steward**, then **the stores officer**, **the watch officer** and the castle's maester, and calls an experienced carpenter, mason or master builder to inspect the works. No northern lord needs to receive a raven to authorize construction on Stark's own castle ground.

During **126 AC, moons four to seven**, they inspect ground, existing stores, well sites, hot-water access, stone sources and craft supplies. Sound granaries and wells may then be built through the remaining moons of 126 and into 127; the baths require distinct tenders and an agreed water and fuel source; the approach is paved in the first working season after stone is stocked, and the yards over 127 and 128. the steward retains the last payment until roofs, doors, water and fire arrangements have been inspected.

**Return to be measured:** the number of quarters of grain demonstrably saved from loss, store capacity actually usable, loss or damage to valuable goods avoided, and any reductions in ordinary castle expense. There is **no promised annual coin return from castle granaries, baths, paving or the school**.

### The hall and annual fair: why it is built

The market hall gives local farmers and merchants a sheltered place to measure, sort, exchange and safeguard goods. The weighhouse makes disputed weights examinable. The warehouses allow merchants to leave goods for a fee instead of carrying them away unsold; the stable permits beasts to rest under cover, if demand warrants fifty places.

First the steward must ascertain whether Winter Town already holds a recognized customary market and whether the lord may add a fair or change its date. In medieval practice a grant or acknowledged custom could be needed, and new fairs were often challenged where they harmed established neighboring markets. The wishes of a northern overlord alone do not settle any required royal right. **There is to be no order forbidding another lord's market**, and no toll until its lawful basis is known.

An initial **fortnight fair around the second moon of 127 AC** is a *possible* first occasion if the rights, date, buildings and neighboring agreements are secured. Another may occur in the second moon of 128 AC. A third fair in the second moon of 129 AC would be **after** the closing of this account. These dates are bookkeeping targets, not proof of a calendar season or of a fair already held.

The proposed charge is **one part in a hundred of the value of taxable goods actually sold**, after exemptions, and **0.0002 dragons per occupied stall-day**. A fair court may hear commercial disputes under the lord's lawful jurisdiction. Two porters, a weighmaster and six temporary watchmen are proposed; **the steward** supervises the account rather than creating a new courts department. No fee is levied on cargo merely passing by. If exemptions are promised to bannermen, their true exempt values must be recorded separately from taxable trade. Before the first fair **the steward** writes down the fair court's rules and, if the lord's court holds the right, an assize of bread and ale; neither costs coin.

### The leather yard

The tanning business takes hides lawfully acquired from Stark livestock and other voluntary sellers. The proposed building consists of one dry shed, **two separate industrial wells**, twenty tons of rubble, **fifty vats provisionally priced at two dragons each**, and drainage and scraping arrangements. Its fixed building charge is **449.60 dragons**, not the former inadequate 323-dragon or 102-dragon estimates. The vats and drains still require a tanner's actual tender. Site the yard **below clean-water intakes**, away from the baths and houses where feasible. Lime, soaked skins, bark and the spent liquor need controlled handling.

The model's demesne share of **23,876 slaughter cattle** suggests a possible **4,775 hides associated with the lord's fifth share**, but **their property rights, existing use and sale are unverified**. If the livestock receipts already include sale of the whole hide, the accounting must give up that raw-hide value before crediting a finished hide. The price book has approximately **0.159042 GD for a raw hide** and **0.397606 GD for a tanned hide**. A proposed commercial agreement earning only half that difference yields **0.119282 GD of gross added margin per actual hide**, before salting, bark, workers, warehousing and losses.

**The large throughput assumption does not pass an ordinary capacity test.** If a bark-tanned hide occupies a pit for **15 moons**, processing **4,775 hides each year** would mean roughly **5,969 hides in cure at once**. With **fifty pits**, that requires almost **120 hides per pit at a time**, before handling space and separate stages. Fifty modest vats should **not** be represented as capable of this without a surveyed pit size and actual tanner's evidence.

### Why the stores are dispersed

A village with a harvest may need grain in that village. Hauling low-priced bulk rye unnecessarily across the North consumes its sale value. The price book sets road carriage at about **0.164220 GD for one long ton hauled a hundred miles**. A quarter of rye in the book weighs **448 pounds, or one-fifth of a long ton**. Freight is therefore **0.032844 GD per quarter per 100 miles**, roughly **52% of its 0.063112 GD sale price**, before sacks, handling or spoilage. This justifies examining local stores instead of taking every quarter to Winterfell.

The **ten rural granaries** therefore offer **10,000 quarters of additional nominal capacity**, entirely distinct from the ten castle granaries. They do not add grain and should not be erected in a village that already has ample sound room.

For a rural store the local reeve must show how much grain is normally held, spoilage, damp, rats and the expected use. The lord's grain and tenant-owned grain need **separate tallies and clear claims of ownership**, even when kept under one roof.

### Terms to negotiate, not to impose

**Common roads, wells, stores, bridges and public landings:** Stark ordinarily bears **six tenths** of the allowed cost; the receiving lord bears **four tenths**, either in coin or in **valued** local material and labor that he may lawfully provide. The lord keeps his courts and ordinary rights and binds himself to upkeep of the completed public asset. No payment is due before his part has been secured and the site examined. The contract states whether the public may use a well or bridge without further charge and who answers for injuries and repair.

**Merchants' houses, cloth works, tanning and other ventures earning money:** Stark can offer a **recoverable advance** in exchange for an identified building or inventory security and an agreed share of **additional net earnings**, after real wages, wood, salt, transport, rent, repairs and ordinary dues. A hard opening offer is **three quarters of new net earnings to Stark and one quarter to the host until the advance is recovered**; thereafter a **half share** for a **fixed and negotiated term**, not forever. A more attractive second offer gives the host a larger share during recoupment or shortens that later term. The arithmetic of a six-year return is an **aim**, not a guarantee.

A different bargain is a **loan at 3% yearly on the amount of principal still owing**, under a stated repayment schedule, without a continuing profit share. **Do not impose both interest and a full profit share unless a genuinely separate consideration is written and accepted.** For example, **250 dragons advanced**, principal paid back in five equal yearly installments of 50, produces annual interest of **7.50, 6, 4.50, 3 and 1.50 dragons**, **22.50 total**, provided each installment is duly paid. Principal returned is **not new profit**.

A commercial owner cannot promise Stark dues that already belong to the Crown or another lord. Preferred access to wool, fish, timber or hides may be negotiated as **first opportunity to buy a stated amount at the ordinary market price**, not an uncompensated claim to a tenth of a fisher's catch. Every material concession extended to one house must be offered on the same grounds to another similarly placed house. **Equal right to apply is not equal amount of building regardless of need.**
