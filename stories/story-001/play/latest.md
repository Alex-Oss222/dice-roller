## Turn 1 | Day 0, 23:10 | Ned's command tent, northern section of the rebel encampment beside the Trident | Elapsed: 5 hours, 10 minutes

### The lords

Ned sends Jory Cassel through the northern tents with one message for each lord: come to the command tent once your own orders are given, and bring no more than one man. Jory says it back to him before he goes. While he is gone Ned's household men carry the folded mail and the gambeson to the back of the tent, set out the stools and the two camp chests that will serve for more, and bring in a cask of ale and whatever cups the baggage can find. There are not enough cups. Two of them are horn.

The lords come in over the better part of an hour. Lord Karstark arrives first, with mud from the horse lines to his ankles, and sits without being asked. Lord Dustin brings a steward with a wax tablet, who stands at the tent wall and does not write anything. Others follow: lords, landed knights, a castellan speaking for a lord who is sick, two sons sent in their fathers' places. The tent is full well before the last of them arrives. The last man stands inside the flap with his cup.

Ned lets them drink before he asks anything. Then he asks, and keeps asking. How many of your horses are sound. What did your outriders see. What are your men saying. The answers come unevenly. Some lords report in numbers and some in complaints. One says his men are ready and in the same breath asks whether anyone has spare bowstrings.

Karstark's is the answer that takes longest. His outriders watered their horses a mile or so below the ford that afternoon, where the river widens. "Wide and slow and not deep," he says. "If they can water a horse there, they can cross one." He wants to know who is watching it. Nobody in the tent knows.

Dustin wants to know whether the Tully levies will stand if the royal knights come at them. He does not look at Ned while he asks it. A younger man says the smallfolk in his company believe the prince has sorcerers with him. Somebody laughs, and then nobody does. A castellan asks, quietly and to the side, whether there has been any word from Winterfell. There has been none since the last raven reached Riverrun.

Ned goes over the plan as the commanders left it at the last council. Robert's heavy horse hold the center at the kingsroad ford. The Vale host holds the right. The northmen hold the left, downstream, on the near bank, and they do not cross until Robert's horns call the whole line forward. He draws it in spilled ale on the lid of a chest because there is no map in the tent. Karstark points to a place below Ned's line. "Here," he says. "That's the water I mean."

Ned tells them he will take the shallows to the commanders tonight. Karstark says he will put a few riders by the water regardless, on his own men's sleep, and Ned does not stop him.

Before they go, Ned gives them the order about rest: every company to its blankets by the second watch, the lords with them, and no man kept up polishing what is already polished. A tired army is a dead army. Dustin repeats the phrase to his steward, who writes that down. Karstark refuses a second cup and leaves. Dustin takes his second cup and stays a while longer.

### The lines

Ned walks the northern lines with Jory a step behind him and no herald. He does not stop at every fire. Where he stops, men get up, or half get up, and he tells them to sit.

He asks them where they are from. A spearman from a holding in the Wolfswood tells him about his sheep, twenty-two of them at last count and one lame. A man from the coast near White Harbor has been fishing the Bite since he was eight and has not been in a boat since the host marched. He wants to know whether the Trident has salmon. Ned does not know. Another man says his wife manages the holding well enough without him, and a little later that the byre roof was letting in water when he left and she cannot get up to it on her own.

Some of them talk easily. More of them answer what they are asked and wait. One gives only his lord's name, twice, as if Ned has asked for it. A boy with an unfinished beard asks whether it is true the prince has dragons. Ned tells him the last dragon died long before either of them was born. The boy nods and does not look satisfied.

By the time Ned reaches the end of the northern lines the captains are passing the rest order down from the lords. It moves unevenly. Karstark's men are already banking their fires. Dustin's are still at their gear when Ned passes, and one of their serjeants is repeating the order to men who look as if they are hearing it for the first time.

### Robert

Robert's pavilion stands in the stormlanders' section behind the center of the camp. The guards know Ned and lift the flap before he reaches it. Inside, an armorer is setting a new rivet in the strap of a breastplate by lamplight, and Robert is on his feet with a cup, walking between the table and the tent pole. The warhammer lies on the table among the cups.

"Ned." Robert embraces him and sends a squire for more wine. He is calm, he says. Calm as a millpond. He says it on his way past the tent pole for the third time.

Ned asks him what he means to do tomorrow. Robert tells him. He will hold the center at the ford with the heavy horse until the royal line is set. Then he will go through it. And somewhere in it he will find Rhaegar. "I'll kill him, Ned. Whatever else happens tomorrow, I mean to kill him."

Ned gives him the warning in plain words. Robert should not be an idiot tomorrow. Ned will not have northern men die for Robert's temper. Robert leads this rebellion, and he needs a calm head for it.

Robert stops walking. For a moment he looks at the armorer, who keeps his eyes on the rivet. Then he laughs, not entirely easily. "An idiot. There's not another man in this camp would say that to me." He drinks. "You sound like Jon."

He will not promise to stay behind the line. "I'm not sitting a horse at the back while another man finds him." Ned presses the point about the northmen, and Robert gives ground on that and nothing else. The horns belong to Jon; Robert will not blow them himself, and no northman crosses the river on Robert's temper alone. "Your wolves stay where Jon puts them until the horns go. Will that do you?" He does not wait for an answer. He sends the squire back out, this time for Jon Arryn and Lord Hoster.

### Jon Arryn and Hoster Tully

Jon Arryn arrives first with a rolled sketch of the river. Hoster Tully comes a quarter of an hour later with two of his knights, whom he leaves outside. Jon spreads the sketch over the cups and weights the corners with them, and the warhammer goes to the floor.

The dispositions do not change. Robert and the stormlanders' heavy horse hold the center at the ford. The Vale host holds the right under Jon. The northmen hold the left, downstream. Hoster's riverlanders stand behind the center as the reserve. Jon goes through the signals himself: one long blast to stand ready, three to advance across the whole front. Nobody crosses before the three except to meet an enemy already over the water.

Ned puts Karstark's shallows on the sketch. Jon does not know the place. Hoster does. "Below the bend. It'll take horse in places when the water's this low, if the riders know where to go." His own men would know it, he says. So might the royal knights of the riverlands.

Jon settles it in a few sentences. Outriders go down to sound the shallows at first light. The northmen watch that stretch of bank through the night and in the morning. If royal horse cross there, the north meets them on the bank and sends word to Jon. It does not go forward until the horns. Ned mentions that Karstark's riders are already at the water. Jon nods and marks the place with his thumbnail.

Hoster says little about any of it. When he speaks it is mostly about his own men: this is their own river and their own lords' land, and he would rather see them in the line than behind it. Jon tells him the reserve is where the battle will be decided. Hoster says he has heard that before, and lets it stand.

Robert listens to most of the meeting from his feet. He agrees to the signals again, in front of Jon this time. He says nothing further about Rhaegar, and nobody asks him.

The meeting breaks up before the third watch. Ned walks back through a quieter camp. Most of the northern fires are down. Two of Dustin's men are still at a grindstone. In the command tent Ned's mail and gambeson lie where the household men put them. He tells Jory to wake him an hour before first light, takes off his boots, and lies down in his campaign clothes with the armor within reach of the bed.
