## Opening: Turn 0

The evening meal is finished. Ned sits with his longsword across his knees, its point angled away from the edge of the ground cloth. The whetstone makes a low, even scrape. At the end of each pass he lifts it clear, turns the blade and begins again near the guard. Grey residue darkens the stone. Beyond the open tent flap, men are still at work among the northern tents.

His mail lies folded beside the padded gambeson. One sleeve has fallen across the leather jerkin, a loose weight of small iron rings. The coif, padded cap and helmet are together; the gloves and their mail coverings lie with the belt and fastening straps. The hauberk reaches well down the thighs, split to allow a man to sit a horse. There is no steel casing for his lower legs. His riding boots are worn at the creases above the ankles. For now he wears his campaign clothes, with the armor close enough to reach.

The heater shield rests nearby with its inner face toward him. Its straps hang loose. The dagger is beside it. Close to the rest of his gear are the saddlebag, folded cloak and blanket, waterskin, mess kit, flint and steel. In the space between his knees and the tent entrance there is room for a man to pass, though not for two men carrying their shields abreast.

A little beyond the flap, a soldier draws a strap through a buckle while another holds the piece of armor steady. The first man pulls it tight, lets it out a hole and pulls again. Farther off comes the short tapping of a tool against metal. Someone has found a fault too small to trouble over on a march and too troublesome to leave until morning. A blade catches the light as its owner tilts it to look along the edge, then disappears behind the man seated in front of him.

Voices carry between the tents. A watch is being repeated to men who have not heard the first telling. One answers too soon and has to listen again. A captain waits for the exchange to finish before passing on, stepping around gear set out in the narrow way. Men look up when others approach, then return to buckles, blades and straps. The work occupies hands that will have little time for it once the host begins to move.

From the direction of the picket lines comes the sound of a horse pulling against its tether, followed by a man's voice. Ned's own riding horse is there with its saddle and tack. The path to it runs through the occupied camp; the animal is beyond the bustle immediately outside his tent. Here the nearest sounds are the scrape of his stone, the small clink of a buckle and feet passing the entrance.

Rhaegar's royal army is nearby. The coming day is expected to bring battle. Between now and then, each contingent has its own preparations to finish, and an order given in this part of the camp still has to reach the men meant to obey it. Robert's men, Jon Arryn's and Hoster Tully's answer to their own commanders. The northern banners are Ned's responsibility.

The man with the buckle finally lowers his hands. His companion stands, shifts his shoulders under the armor and bends once at the waist. They exchange a few words too low to reach the tent. Ned's own hauberk remains folded beside the gambeson. The longsword lies across his knees, the whetstone near its edge, while the camp's preparations continue outside.
