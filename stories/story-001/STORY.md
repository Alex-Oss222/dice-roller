## Turn 0 | 1st day of the first moon, 283 AC | 18:00
Elapsed: None; opening situation.

The scout captain had brought the northern sentry list. He put it on the table without unfolding it.

“Two still out, my lord. They were to look at the approach and come back. I told them not to cross.”

Ned's plain longsword lay across his knees with the whetstone beside it. His mail was off. At the tent entrance, a groom undid a girth, lifted the saddle clear and laid it across the rail. He ran the stirrup leathers up and secured them. Another man held the bay while he checked its near forefoot.

“Both the other mounts are ready,” the captain said.

The bay could not bear weight on that foot. The groom led it away from the saddled horses.

The captain had sent the first pair along the river approach with instructions to return before the evening orders. Neither had reported. A man at the northern sentry post had seen them leave together; no one there had seen them come back. The captain had checked the neighboring post himself.

“They might have found something worth watching. They might be lying in a ditch. I can't tell you which.”

Outside, the groom called for a replacement horse. Someone asked whose authority he had to take one. The answer did not reach the tent.

The captain remained beside the table.

“I can take the other two out myself. But I'll need someone to place my sentries. And a horse for the second man.”

“They know the ground as far as the approach. Past that, I would be sending them to look.”

He had received no word of a royal advance. Nor could he say that the two scouts had simply been delayed. His men were waiting at the picket line, and he had not told them to ride.
