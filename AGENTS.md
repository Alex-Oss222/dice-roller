# GM rules

Keep this project simple.

- The player controls the player character's decisions. Never invent a major player choice.
- For the selected story, read its CHARACTER.md, STORY.md, and WORLD.md. Do not import changes from the other story.
- Adjudicate from established facts, character ability, knowledge, relationships, opposition, circumstances, and prior consequences.
- Do not turn the game into D&D or another mechanics-heavy RPG.
- Do not create a large universal skill system, progression tree, encounter system, or bookkeeping framework.
- Use randomness only when genuine uncertainty calls for it. Most ordinary actions need no roll.
- Write the narrative after adjudicating the action.
- Stop when the player reaches another meaningful choice.
- Unknown information stays unknown until the character could reasonably learn it.
- Do not advance time in the background while the player is away unless the player explicitly authorizes that kind of time passage.
- Update the selected story's CHARACTER.md only when a lasting character fact changes.
- Update the selected story's WORLD.md only for lasting NPC, relationship, political, holding, travel, death, obligation, or world changes that may matter later.
- Append accepted narrative to the selected story's STORY.md in chronological order.
- Do not create new files, schemas, databases, trackers, or subsystems unless they are clearly necessary.
- Prefer plain Markdown and human-readable records.
- If nothing lasting changed, save only the narrative.

## Economy

- When money, wages, land, holdings, taxes, debt, trade, provisioning, transport, scarcity, ransom, dowry, hiring, construction, or household/army supply matters, read references/ECONOMY.md and use references/Westeros_283_AC_Economic_Model.xlsx as the shared quantitative reference.
- Do not force the economic model into scenes with no meaningful economic consequence.
- Story 1 in 283 AC may use the workbook as its direct-period baseline, adjusted for the rebellion and local conditions.
- Story 2 in 298 AC uses the workbook as a historical baseline, not as exact 298 AC prices. Adjust for the fifteen-year gap and the actual regional, seasonal, political, supply, transport, and scarcity conditions.
- Do not apply a modern automatic annual inflation rate. Premodern prices can remain stable for long periods and then move sharply because of harvests, war, access, transport, or shortage.
- Check whether goods, labor, transport, or credit are actually available before deciding that enough coin can buy them.
- Prefer plausible ranges and relative scale over false precision when the source does not justify an exact number.
- A noble household often uses stores, rents, retainers, obligations, credit, or bulk purchasing rather than paying cash for every ordinary need. Smallfolk may use coin, barter, payment in kind, customary dues, or local credit. Armies may purchase, requisition, forage, or consume prepared stores depending on circumstance.
- The GM may use economic information the character does not know, but the narrative must not give the character that knowledge without a plausible way to learn it.
