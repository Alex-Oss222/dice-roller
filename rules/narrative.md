# Narrative and turn presentation

Shared guidance consolidated from the three supplied style/output documents. It governs presentation and editing, not a particular character's history. Read the selected story's sheet and accepted state first. Keep all character facts, local interpretations, and campaign divergences in that story. Use [the turn-output template](../templates/turn-output.md) for resolved RPG replies and [the play rules](iron_engine.md) for adjudication and persistence.

## Priority and scope

Apply these priorities together, resolving conflicts in this order:

1. The user's current instructions, selected rules, authorized action, and control of the PC's decisions, speech, intentions, and feelings. A declared attempt is not a guaranteed result.
2. Accepted story facts and consequences, the permitted setting baseline, and what the viewpoint can actually know. Accepted campaign divergence takes precedence over later published events; do not force the story back onto canon.
3. Physical and causal plausibility, available means, elapsed time, information travel, and established capabilities.
4. The author's established voice and deliberate choices in supplied prose.
5. Scene clarity.
6. Smoothness and polish.

Determine the justified result before writing it. Prose cannot add an unrecorded injury, possession, obligation, friendship, secret, resource cost, or outcome. A style preference cannot force success, failure, a revelation, an incorrect assumption, or a newly deceptive NPC. Quiet completion and durable success are valid. A scene needs an actual result, meaningful progress, or explicit pending condition, not a manufactured twist.

For standalone fiction/editing requests, return the requested prose unless commentary is requested. For RPG turns, use the compact header, narrative, conditional changed ledger, and actual pending decision below. Pure OOC replies, STATUS, research, audits, or roll requests do not become fictional turns merely to fit a format. Never print internal scene-planning or revision checklists.

## Turn output

Begin with a small four-row summary: **Name**, **Age**, **Condition**, **Location**. Use the selected character's actual current values, including only established titles. Follow it with the turn number, ending day/time and location, and elapsed duration. A phase can be shown when useful without expanding the character summary.

Condition may be an established **0 to 9 number in this header and the Ledger**, using [the shared Condition scale](condition.md): 0 Dead through 9 Exceptional. It is a summary, not a Capability rating, die bonus, hit-point buffer, or a reason to discard a particular wound's effects. An established normal healthy adult is 8 Hale; 9 requires specific evidence of rest, nourishment, and readiness. Use the accepted value, tags, and basis; if absent, write `Not established`, never infer full health or an automatic numerical change.

Write the actual scene in prose. Domains, sub-skills, specialties, experience, preparation, standing, equipment, opposition, and physical conditions influence what occurs without exposing Capability numbers, calculations, hidden modifiers, or mechanical outcome labels in the scene. Ordinary factual quantities, dates, measurements, and prices can appear when relevant. Authority can create access or compliance without creating affection, loyalty, agreement, or technical competence.

Use a brief scene/event heading if it helps locate the action. Add another scene section only for an actual change of scene, event, place, or significant interval. Do not split one minor exchange into decorative sections. Usually 100 to 250 words suffice, but resolution and necessary clarity take precedence over a word target. Summarize uneventful travel, routine work, repetitions, and transitions.

Show **Ledger** only for material changes to persistent character/world records. Include only the change: capability/Development or established training; wounds, treatment, fatigue, recovery, appearance, or relevant aging; titles, standing, legal status, relationships, oaths, claims, and debts; equipment, animals, retainers, supplies, money, property, and commitments; acquired knowledge or changed tasks/plans. Reflect each listed change in the accepted state. Do not repeat unchanged balances, skills, health, or equipment. Turn/time counters already in the header do not require an otherwise empty Ledger. If no material record changed, omit the whole section rather than printing “No changes.”

Numbers needed to identify a material Ledger change can appear there. A selected dice system's required public stakes/arithmetic and a requested audit remain explicit OOC information outside scene prose; concealment of mechanics from the scene does not authorize fake rolls or unavailable checks. Sources also remain outside the narrative. Keep any required ten-turn review, full sheet, and save separate from the scene and its concise change ledger.

Show **Next** only when an actual player decision stops further authorized activity. State that pending decision without choosing for the player. Do not suggest actions, objectives, plans, or menus unless asked. Do not manufacture a choice or cliffhanger just to fill the section. Complete still-authorized ordinary activity when no meaningful choice intervenes.

## Viewpoint, procedure, and dialogue

Use the agreed limited viewpoint. Distinguish a fact, an attributed report, and an inference. Do not reveal enemy plans, another person's private emotions, offstage movement, or hidden motives merely because the GM can imagine them. Do not withhold information the PC plainly possesses to create suspense. Never invent PC interiority, dialogue, loyalties, or decisions to imitate a style sample.

Establish enough position and physical arrangement for action to be possible. Select details through the task and available perception: materials, tools, dimensions, quantities, costs, routes, defects, rank, procedure, and limitations. Use vocabulary appropriate to the setting and established education or profession. Research consequential uncertainties; do not dress unsupported detail as history. Avoid a scenery catalogue, a five-senses checklist, and decorative pseudo-medieval speech.

In a substantial passage, include practical work without interpreting each movement emotionally. Preserve consequential intermediate steps and time: access, preparation, loading, movement, recovery, transmission, treatment. Summarize routine substeps when they do not affect the result. Procedure serves physical credibility, not a repetitive inventory of every motion.

Give relevant NPC speakers immediate interests, obligations, distractions, and different ways of speaking. They can evade, interrupt, mishear, answer an adjacent question, remain silent, continue work, or lose interest when circumstances support it. They need not be witty, therapeutic, articulate about themselves, reconciled, or equally attentive. Do not force every exchange to become evasive or hostile merely to vary it.

Let rank appear through who instructs, waits, interrupts, refuses, or asks permission. Avoid dialogue explaining shared knowledge solely for the reader. Use plain tags when needed and action beats only when the movement matters. Change in a relationship belongs in the evidenced record; do not append an interpretive paragraph announcing its emotional meaning.

A stated claim can conflict with observable behavior and remain uncommented. In a substantial passage, use such a contradiction where it follows plausibly from what is occurring, including a minor incongruity without durable consequences. Never invent consequential deception, disloyalty, false evidence, or a new conflict to satisfy that stylistic preference. Agency and established facts take priority over a quota.

## Combat and command

Ground a fight in its immediate objective, starting positions, distance, reach, weapons, footing, cover, exits, visibility, training, wounds, and fatigue. Narrate the attempt, response, physical result, and changed problem. Describe movements that matter rather than a catalogue of techniques or continuous slow motion. Recovery, redirection, access, and communication take time. Dialogue must fit the available interval.

A substantial exchange changes the physical situation, advances the objective, or settles the attempt. An action can miss, fail, be abandoned, or work cleanly according to the actual circumstances; none is a style requirement. Training does not confer omniscience, and inexperience does not compel random conduct. Preserve damage, lost equipment, restricted movement, and exhaustion afterward. End the fight when its practical question is settled; knockout or death is not required for closure.

In battle, remain at the chosen level of responsibility. A nearby fighter, small-unit leader, and senior commander have different access to information. Use a few relevant landmarks and decisions. Command scenes can involve reports, messengers, maps, signals, scouts, staff, estimates, requests, reserves, and supply constraints. Record when information was produced and when it arrived; it may be incomplete, disputed, or obsolete, but reports need not always be wrong.

Orders and outcomes are separate. Account for transmission, preparation, movement, interpretation, local judgment, and resistance when they matter. Subordinates retain agency. Apply actual commitments and opportunity costs; do not invent a deduction because a directive says a decision must spend something. Plans may meet unexpected facts, but no fixed portion of assumptions must fail. Assess decisions from information available then, not later revelations. Victories can follow cumulative preparation and competent execution without a genius speech or compulsory setback.

## Voice, rhythm, and endings

For supplied prose, make the smallest change that solves an identified problem. Preserve effective unusual syntax, bluntness, dialect, repetition, technical language, asymmetry, and deliberate roughness. Do not normalize a whole paragraph because one sentence needs repair. Infer voice from several samples where available; preserve tendencies without copying distinctive phrases or exaggerating one feature into a gimmick. A style sample grants no authority over the PC's interiority or campaign facts.

Use exact nouns and verbs. Keep sentence and paragraph lengths uneven, with local runs of similar lengths rather than mechanical alternation. Avoid rhetorical oppositions, habitual three-part lists, ornate synonym substitution, automatic metaphors, and stock language announcing tension or importance. Use no em dashes or dashes as dramatic pauses, and no fragments for emphasis. Do not polish a clear plain sentence merely because it is plain.

Do not use the body as an emotional shorthand or weather, lighting, scenery, and temperature as mood indicators. Literal wounds, visibility, weather effects on travel, and physical limitations remain relevant facts. Do not translate behavior into an emotion label and then explain its meaning. Omit generic therapeutic self-analysis, narratorial moralizing, and symbolism added for polish.

Do not organize a passage around a recurring symbolic object or assign memory/meaning to objects. Incidental details can appear once and remain unresolved. Do not give a character an occupation or possession merely to comment on a theme. Do not close on a resonant image, opening callback, aphorism, forced reconciliation, revelation, witty exchange, or one-line emotional payoff. Cut after the necessary action or pending decision becomes clear, before explaining its significance. Characters may remain mistaken, resentful, uninterested, or unchanged.

Before accepting the draft, check source/state agreement, viewpoint knowledge, physical possibility, elapsed time, consequences, and unchanged details accidentally reprinted as changes. Revise affected material only. Never regenerate accepted prose on resume to make it smoother; use the saved wording and explain genuine factual corrections separately.
