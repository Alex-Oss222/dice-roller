# Combat and battle narrative

Read this only for fighting, battle, siege, pursuit, or consequential military movement. Apply the general adjudication principle in `AGENTS.md` and the prose rules in `NARRATIVE.md`.

## Battles and armies

- Begin with objectives, ground, forces, deployment, supply, routes, reserves, communications, command relationships, and ways to withdraw.
- Armies are organized groups. A commander's intention must be carried out by people with their own training, condition, knowledge, and cohesion.
- Orders and information take time to travel and may be misunderstood, delayed, ignored, or overtaken by events.
- Crossings, roads, baggage, animals, provisions, fatigue, weather as a physical condition, formation, morale, and local knowledge affect what is possible.
- Follow decisions, reactions, and disruptions as they occur. A commander acts on reports and observation, not an omniscient map.
- A famous person's death does not automatically end a battle.
- Retreat, pursuit, surrender, capture, straggling, reorganization, and supply after the fight are part of the outcome.

## Strength and losses

- Use the force present at the relevant date and place, including campaign changes. A realm's potential levy, several combined armies, and one field host are different quantities. Distinguish a muster from a scout's estimate; establish a missing count at book scale when needed.
- Losses follow the engagement and its aftermath. Distinguish killed, wounded, captured, missing or dispersed, and those fit for duty. Defeat or the destruction of an organized force does not itself specify how many died. Avoid double-counting a wounded man who later dies.
- Save material losses and remaining available strength in `WORLD.md`, with the report's date and scope. Account for detachments, reinforcements, recoveries, desertions, and returning stragglers when relevant. Correct an estimate explicitly when better information arrives.
- Let losses affect subsequent events through reduced formations, absent officers, wounded needing transport, prisoners, or changed orders. Use what matters to the scene without displaying a compulsory casualty ledger during fighting.

## Individual fighting

- Track distance, space, footing, weapons, armor, numbers, position, fatigue, injury, and routes of escape.
- Show skill through timing, positioning, restraint, recovery, use of terrain, and recognition of danger.
- Skill is specific. Swordsmanship does not automatically grant wrestling, mounted fighting, battlefield awareness, or command ability.
- Multiple opponents remain present; they do not wait their turn to attack.
- Being outnumbered is dangerous. Terrain or a restricted approach can help, but does not guarantee survival.
- Armor protects without making the wearer invulnerable or helpless.
- Injuries have physical consequences and persist when relevant.
- Select actions that change the situation rather than transcribing every movement.

## Viewpoint and prose

Give the viewpoint character only what they can perceive or learn. Wider facts can emerge through scouts, messengers, prisoners, counts, and aftermath. Preserve the difference between an obvious outcome and an unfinished final count.

Combat remains fiction. Do not switch to an omniscient tactical report or show rolls, hit points, damage scores, initiative, difficulty, or explanations of adjudication.
