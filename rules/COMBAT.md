# Combat and battle narrative

Read this only when the turn actually involves combat, a battle, siege, pursuit, or consequential military movement.

These rules supplement `NARRATIVE.md`. They do not create a combat minigame.

## Battles and armies

- Begin with objectives, ground, forces, deployment, supply, routes, reserves, communications, command relationships, and ways to withdraw.
- Armies are organized groups, not thousands of simultaneous duels.
- Orders take time to travel and may be misunderstood, delayed, ignored, or overtaken by events.
- Terrain, crossings, roads, baggage, animals, food, water, fatigue, weather as a physical condition, formation, morale, and local knowledge can change what is possible.
- A commander acts on reports and observation, not an omniscient map.
- Let the battle develop as a chain of decisions and disruptions. Do not predetermine a heroic climax.
- A famous person's death does not automatically end a battle.
- Retreat, pursuit, surrender, capture, straggling, reorganization, and supply after the fight are part of the military outcome.

## Individual fighting

- Track distance, space, footing, weapons, armor, numbers, position, fatigue, injury, and routes of escape.
- Show skill through decisions: timing, distance, positioning, restraint, recovery, use of terrain, and recognition of danger.
- Skill is specific. Swordsmanship does not automatically grant wrestling, mounted fighting, battlefield awareness, or command ability.
- Multiple opponents remain present. Do not make attackers vanish while waiting for their turn.
- Being outnumbered is dangerous. Terrain or a restricted approach can help, but does not guarantee survival.
- Armor protects without making the wearer either invulnerable or helpless.
- Injuries have physical consequences and persist when relevant.
- Select the actions that change the situation. Do not transcribe every parry, cut, step, and feint.

## Viewpoint

The reader receives battlefield information through what the viewpoint character can actually perceive or learn. Wider military facts can be established later through reports, scouts, messengers, prisoners, counts, or aftermath.

Do not switch into an omniscient tactical report merely because combat began.

## Prose

Combat remains narrative. Never show rolls, checks, hit points, damage numbers, initiative, difficulty, or mechanical success/failure language.
