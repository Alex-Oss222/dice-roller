# Adjudicated resolution, use-based progression and campaign review in solo/GM-less RPGs and LLM implementations

Sourcing note: the research sandbox's egress proxy blocked most primary hosts (arxiv.org, bladesinthedark.com, redcap.org, forums.burningwheel.com, wikipedia, roll20, substack, wordpress, blogspot, obsidian publish, traveller-srd.com). Findings below therefore come from (a) pages that were fully fetched (GitHub READMEs/SRD mirrors, one Pendragon reference repo) and (b) search-result extracts of the named sources. Items drawn only from search extracts are marked "(search extract)" so the writer can weigh them; page numbers are given only where the source itself gave them.

## Key question 1: Which published systems tie advancement to elapsed in-fiction time and actual practice, and what are their rates and safeguards against free advancement?

### Takeaway
Every established "use/time" system pairs a *gate* (a logged test, a marked check, a spent season/week, or a spent roll) with a *rate limiter* (one skill per week, one train per track per downtime, one activity per season, chance-of-failure rolls that get harder as the skill rises). None allow advancement merely because time passed; the time must be spent on a named activity, and most make the improvement roll fail more often as ratings climb.

### Cited Findings
- **Burning Wheel** (Burning Wheel Gold; Player's Procedure Guide V2): advancement requires filling required test counts in three categories, Routine, Difficult and Challenging; "All graduated tests count as routine tests"; requirements scale by exponent (e.g. exponent 1 and 2: 1 routine + 1 difficult; exponent 3: 2 routine + 1 challenging); practice rule: "If you have logged the required hours of practice in a skill or stat, you may mark the related test." (search extract) — [Burning Wheel Player's Procedure Guide V2](http://questingblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Burning-Wheel-Procedural-Guide-V2.pdf); mirror [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/343140276/Burning-Wheel-Procedural-Guide-V2)
- Burning Wheel was "designed for prolonged campaign play and character development" (search extract) — [reddicediaries, RPGaDay 2023](https://reddicediaries.substack.com/p/rpgaday-2023-6)
- **Mythras / RuneQuest 6 lineage** (Mythras SRD, "Game System" chapter): "Games Masters dispense Experience Rolls at an appropriate juncture in the campaign: at the end of every successful scenario or storyline; or after perhaps two or three sessions of play if the story is a long one." Any skill can be raised by spending one Experience Roll: "roll 1d100 ... The character's INT is added to the roll. If the number rolled is equal to or greater than the skill being improved, it increases by 1d4+1%. If the number rolled is less than the skill selected, the skill still increases, but only by 1%." Learning a wholly new skill: "the character must spend an entire month of study and practice to garner a basic grounding in that skill (learn at Base Level). This costs 3 Experience Rolls plus whatever in-game costs are required." (search extract; the repo exists but the chapter path could not be fetched) — [Mythras SRD, Game System](https://github.com/raleel/mythras-srd/blob/main/0004_Game_System.md)
- **HarnMaster** (3rd ed. lineage): "Spend the SDR [Skill Development Roll] and roll D100 plus the applicable skill's SB. If the result is greater than the skill, then the skill goes up one point"; specialties improve by two; "Every 10 skill maintenance points allows the player to make 1 skill development roll"; SB caps maximum skill at 100 + SB (search extracts) — [Bat in the Attic: A walk through HarnMaster - Skills](https://batintheattic.blogspot.com/2020/04/a-walk-through-harnmaster-skills.html); [HarnMaster Player's Guide (usermode.org)](https://www.usermode.org/docs/playersguide.html)
- **Pendragon** (Winter Phase, annual): "Every skill that can change as a result of learning is marked on the character sheet by having a box near it. This box is checked when the character gains significant experience during an adventure with a skill." At winter, "For every skill you have ticked (marked), you roll a D20. If you get higher than the listed number, it goes up by one! (For values of 20 or more, rolling a 20 will make it go up by one.)" and "Experience checks for skills are intended to be very difficult to gain, and are not automatic whenever a success is achieved." (search extracts) — [MK RPG Club, Pendragon winter phase summary](https://www.mk-rpg.org.uk/Pendragon_winter_phase_summary); [Pendragon 6e Let's Study part 7](https://philgamer.wordpress.com/2024/06/20/lets-study-pendragon-6e-core-rulebook-part-7-the-winter-phase/)
- Pendragon training step (fetched reference repo; edition not stated in the repo): the Winter Phase has nine sequential steps; Step 2 experience rolls ("A successful roll increases the checked value by 1 point"); Step 7 Training and Practice, choose one: "Gain 1d6 + 1 Points in Skills (To Max 15)" spread over several skills, or "Gain 1 Point in a Skill (To Max 20)", or improve one attribute/trait/passion. Step 3 aging rolls with "Statistics Lost" on failure; Step 4 economic circumstances from Impoverished to Superlative; Step 6 family events (marriage, childbirth, child survival) — [DavidTPate/pendragon rules reference](https://github.com/DavidTPate/pendragon)
- "During the Winter Phase, each knight can train one skill, trait, or passion." (search extract) — [Quest Portal, Mastering the Winter Phase](https://www.questportal.com/blog/mastering-the-winter-phase-in-pendragon)
- **Ars Magica 5e** (ch. 10 Long-Term Events): "experience points are awarded every season (three months), based on the character's activities during that season"; a character "gains experience points in an Art or Ability when she spends seasons studying it." Practice: "When spending a season practicing without aid from others or access to books/vis, you typically gain 4 XP, though this might be modified down to 3 or up to as much as 8 for certain types of practice. However, Hermetic Arts cannot be increased by simple practice." Exposure: "A character may gain experience from exposure in all and only those seasons in which she does not gain experience points from any other form of advancement." Training: "The student has a Source Quality three higher than the teacher's score ... The master must have a higher score than the apprentice." (search extracts) — [Project Redcap, ArM5 Chapter Ten: Long-Term Events](https://www.redcap.org/page/Ars_Magica_5E_Standard_Edition,_Chapter_Ten:_Long-Term_Events); [Project Redcap, Experience points](http://www.redcap.org/page/Experience_points); [Ars Magica Definitive Edition manuscript, Experience and Advancement](https://publish.obsidian.md/ars-magica-definitive-manuscript/10+Long+Term+Events/Experience+and+Advancement)
- **Traveller (Mongoose 2e)**: "To increase a skill, a character must train for a number of weeks equal to his current skill total plus the desired level of the skill. So, to advance from Pilot 2 to Pilot 3 with a current skill total of 3 would take (three, plus three) six weeks." Skill Total = sum of all skill levels (level 0 counts as zero). "A character may only train one skill in a given week." (search extract) — [Traveller SRD, Skills](https://www.traveller-srd.com/core-rules/skills/); forum discussion of treating improvement as a task: [Mongoose forum](https://forum.mongoosepublishing.com/threads/skill-and-characteristic-improvement-as-a-task.123873/)
- **Blades in the Dark** (SRD, Downtime): Train activity: "When you spend time in training during downtime, you mark 1 XP on the XP track for an attribute or playbook advancement, or +1 XP (2 total) if you have the appropriate crew Training upgrade unlocked." "A given xp track can be trained only once per downtime phase." Long-term projects: "A basic long-term project (like tinkering up a new feature for a device) is eight segments. Truly long-term projects (like creating a new designer drug) can be two, three, or even four clocks"; ticks per downtime roll: 1-3 = one segment, 4/5 = two, 6 = three, critical = five (fetched SRD mirror plus search extract) — [Blades in the Dark SRD (GitHub mirror)](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md); [bladesinthedark.com Downtime Activities](https://bladesinthedark.com/downtime-activities)
- **AD&D 1e DMG p. 37** (Gygax): "Game time is of utmost importance. Failure to keep careful track of time expenditure by player characters will result in many anomalies in the game. The stricture of time is what makes recovery of hit points meaningful ... Time is also considered in gaining levels and learning new languages and more. All of these demands upon game time force choices upon player characters ... YOU CAN NOT HAVE A MEANINGFUL CAMPAIGN IF STRICT TIME RECORDS ARE NOT KEPT." (search extract quoting the DMG) — [Seed of Worlds, Tracking "Strict Time Records"](https://seedofworlds.blogspot.com/2024/03/tracking-strict-time-records-in-campaign.html)
- **Ironsworn/Starforged**: experience is earned by completing vows/progress tracks rather than by time; ranks troublesome/dangerous/formidable/extreme/epic mark 1 tick / 2 ticks / 1 box / 2 boxes / 3 boxes per progress mark on a ten-box track (search extract) — [Starforged SRD, Quest moves](https://rsek.github.io/starforged-srd/moves/quest); [Prof E's Blog on progress moves](https://exuberant-engelhardtlm1.wordpress.com/2022/09/01/ironsworn-starforged-progress-move-thoughts/)

### Inferences
- Two safeguard families recur: (1) *gating by evidence* (BW logged tests/hours, Pendragon check boxes, HarnMaster maintenance points, Mythras rolls dispensed only at story ends) and (2) *diminishing returns* (Pendragon d20-over-skill, Mythras d100+INT ≥ skill, HarnMaster d100+SB > skill, Traveller weeks growing with total skill, Ars Magica pyramid costs implied by seasonal XP). A home-grown "time + use" rule that lacks one of these two families will drift toward free advancement.
- Systems that advance by calendar (Pendragon yearly, Ars Magica seasonal, Traveller weekly, Blades per-downtime) all cap *how many things* may advance per period (one skill/week; one train per track; one activity per season; one training choice per winter). A per-interval cap is the practical equivalent of "no automatic skill gain".
- Mythras's "after two or three sessions" and Pendragon's "at winter" both make advancement a scheduled *review event*, not something resolved inside a scene.

### Gaps
- Exact Burning Wheel practice-time table (hours per Routine/Difficult/Challenging test by skill type) could not be retrieved; forums.burningwheel.com and the PDF host were blocked.
- Ars Magica exact Exposure (commonly 2 XP) and Adventure XP ranges and the XP pyramid costs could not be confirmed from a fetched page; only Practice (4, range 3-8) was confirmed via extract.
- Mythras difficulty grade multipliers and the exact SRD page for Experience Rolls were not fetched (the raw file path returned 404).
- Pendragon 6e-specific page numbers and the edition of the fetched reference repo are unknown.

## Key question 2: How do systems model NPC competence so a contest is judged from both sides?

### Takeaway
Established practice gives the NPC a rating of its own and a procedure (opposed roll, success-level comparison, faction Tier/quality, progress-track rank) that lets the stronger side usually win; where the fiction makes an attempt impossible, the GM/oracle is told not to roll at all. Practitioners warn that solo play collapses when only the PC rolls and every failure is "nothing happens".

### Cited Findings
- **Blades in the Dark** models opposition by faction Tier and by scale/quality/potency modifying *effect* before any dice are rolled; the SRD example: "The Occult gang is Tier III. Thorn is outclassed in quality, so her effect will be limited on the lock." Position and effect are set by the GM from the fiction: "The GM looks at the profiles for the positions below and picks one that most closely matches the situation at hand"; larger opposing forces reduce effect. Factions have their own agendas: "When the PCs have downtime, the GM ticks forward the faction clocks that they're interested in." — [Blades in the Dark SRD (GitHub mirror)](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md)
- **Ironsworn/Starforged** rates foes and quests by rank (troublesome to epic), which sets how slowly the PC's progress track fills; a Starforged combat discussion notes that an extreme-rank foe fills "half a box every time you mark progress" while inflicting dangerous-rank harm (search extracts) — [Starforged SRD, Quest moves](https://rsek.github.io/starforged-srd/moves/quest); [Chamomile, Starforged Combat](https://www.patreon.com/ChamomileHasAPatreon/posts/starforged-109108512)
- **Mythras**: skills are percentile and contests are opposed/differential rolls; Experience Rolls also make higher skills harder to raise (search extract; the opposed-roll section itself was not fetched) — [Mythras SRD, Game System](https://github.com/raleel/mythras-srd/blob/main/0004_Game_System.md)
- **Pendragon** uses opposed d20 rolls against NPC skill ratings; a Chaosium forum archive thread titled "Simplifying NPCs" exists in the Pendragon forum archive (content not fetched) — [Nocturnal Media/Chaosium forum archive](https://greathall.chaosium.com/Pendragon%20Forum%20Archive/index.php/t-2710.html)
- **Solo-play design critique**: one approach "is to only make rolls for your character, not for NPCs ... the combat itself is dictated solely by the rolls of your character," which is fast but raises balance concerns; players report it is "annoying when an experienced character makes a noob mistake that breaks immersion if the character is supposed to be one of the best," and house rules are used to fix this (search extracts from itch.io discussion threads) — [itch.io post 9932305](https://itch.io/post/9932305); [itch.io post 9932921](https://itch.io/post/9932921)
- Solo combat advice: "A failed roll should not simply mean 'nothing happens'—it should make the next decision more interesting. Solo combat often becomes better when it asks fewer questions, creates stronger consequences, and keeps the situation changing." (search extract) — [Wilona's Cave, Why Solo RPG Combat Often Falls Flat](https://wilonascave.substack.com/p/why-solo-rpg-combat-often-falls-flat)
- Solo NPC design advice recommends giving NPCs their own goals so they act on the player rather than merely react (search extract) — [Croaker RPGs, Create Exciting NPCs for your Solo Games](https://croakerrpgs.substack.com/p/create-exciting-npcs-for-your-solo)
- **LLM engine example (fetched)**: Cyb3rRon1n/oracle keeps NPC stats, AC, proficiency, spell slots and death saves in a Python engine; "The model narrates and adjudicates through tool calls (`update_character`, `update_world`, `request_roll`, `lookup_rule`); it never gets trusted with arithmetic." — [Cyb3rRon1n/oracle](https://github.com/Cyb3rRon1n/oracle)

### Inferences
- The common thread across Blades (Tier/quality set effect *before* the roll), Ironsworn (rank throttles progress) and percentile systems (NPC has its own skill number) is that NPC competence is expressed as a number or grade that mechanically constrains the PC's best case. A "superior political operator" is handled in Blades terms as higher Tier/quality giving the PC *limited* effect, or in percentile terms as an opposed roll the NPC is favoured to win.
- "The fiction says no" is operationalised in Blades as the GM picking position/effect (including zero effect) from the situation rather than letting the dice decide everything; the same logic underlies the repository contract's rule that ability ratings constrain the scene before it is written.
- Practitioners' main warning is not that PCs are too strong but that PC-only rolling with null failures removes opposition; the fix is NPC agency (goals, clocks) plus consequential failure.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gave an explicit rule text for "don't roll if impossible" (Apocalypse World/Blades "if there's no chance of success, don't roll" style); egress blocks prevented confirming wording.
- Mythras differential-roll procedure and Pendragon opposed-roll tie rules were not retrievable in quotable form.
- No source specifically about political/social contests against superior NPCs was found; evidence is generalised from combat and heist mechanics.

## Key question 3: What campaign bookkeeping structures are recommended for long solo campaigns, and what do they track versus deliberately drop?

### Takeaway
Long-form solo structures keep short, refreshed lists (open threads, active NPCs/factions, clocks, a calendar/timeline) and update them at fixed points (end of scene, downtime, winter, season); they deliberately drop resolved threads, low-relevance NPCs and prose detail from the working set, keeping that in a separate journal.

### Cited Findings
- **Mythic GME 2e**: "The Threads List is for adventure objectives while the Characters List is for important Non-Player Characters (NPCs)"; the Characters list also tracks "factions, places, objects"; "The end of a Scene is when Bookkeeping takes place: updating the Threads and Characters Lists and adjusting the Chaos Factor"; "you can add an additional entry for a Thread or Character that was highly relevant in the scene (up to 3 entries total for an element). This increases its chance of being selected in future Random Events"; lists "function as dynamic random tables" (search extracts) — [Mythic GME 2e Adventure Lists (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/693163042/Mythic-GME-2e-Adventure-Lists); [Setting Up and Using Lists, Mythic GME Apps Guide](https://jasonholtdigital.com/resources/setting-up-and-using-lists/); [Word Mill Games, Mythic GME](https://www.wordmillgames.com/page/mythic-gme.html)
- **Blades in the Dark**: faction clocks tick during downtime so the city advances off-screen; long-term projects are 8-segment clocks, chained for truly long projects — [Blades SRD mirror](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md)
- **Pendragon**: the Winter Phase is a nine-step annual review covering experience, aging, economics, family events and training — [DavidTPate/pendragon](https://github.com/DavidTPate/pendragon); [Quest Portal blog](https://www.questportal.com/blog/mastering-the-winter-phase-in-pendragon)
- **Ars Magica**: one advancement activity per season; exposure only in seasons with no other advancement (search extract) — [Project Redcap ch. 10](https://www.redcap.org/page/Ars_Magica_5E_Standard_Edition,_Chapter_Ten:_Long-Term_Events)
- **1e strict time records** (DMG p. 37) justify a campaign calendar by healing, item creation, level gain and language learning all consuming tracked time (search extract) — [Seed of Worlds](https://seedofworlds.blogspot.com/2024/03/tracking-strict-time-records-in-campaign.html)
- **Solo journaling practice**: "At the beginning of each session, you should skim notes or journal to get centered"; "Dedicate a constantly updating page or section ... that lists your current quest threads, just like a computer game would," with a main-quest line and a side-quest section; per session record "scenes (a short title and 2-5 lines of what actually happened), choices ... and threads (clues, unresolved situations, promises to yourself to follow up)"; "Use a timeline spread with a quick bulleted list of major in-world events" (search extracts) — [Soloist, 8 Journaling Tips for Solo RPGs](https://soloist.substack.com/p/8-journaling-tips-for-solo-rpgs); [Making Mythology, solo rpg journaling](https://makingmythology.substack.com/p/solo-rpg-journalingeasier-than-you); [Castle Grief, 6 Fixes For Your Solo TTRPG Campaign](https://castlegrief.substack.com/p/6-fixes-for-your-solo-ttrpg-campaign)
- **Ironsworn Companion (fetched)** separates lightweight auto-generated logs from prose: it auto-logs "character creation, NPC encounters, vow progress, combat outcomes, and delve activities" as timestamped entries, tracks vow/journey/combat tracks by rank, bonds, debilities and assets, and states "Logs are not intended for prose or journaling" (journal is a separate markdown feature) — [gcoulby/IronswornCompanion](https://github.com/gcoulby/IronswornCompanion)
- **LLM engine (fetched)**: Cyb3rRon1n/oracle keeps "Character sheets, world state, turn order, the campaign clock" in the engine, not in the model's context — [Cyb3rRon1n/oracle](https://github.com/Cyb3rRon1n/oracle)

### Inferences
- The consensus working set is: open threads, active characters/factions (weighted by recent relevance), clocks/progress tracks, and a calendar; everything else goes to a journal that is skimmed, not loaded. Mythic's "up to 3 entries" weighting and end-of-scene pruning are the closest published analogue to a "working focus" that retires settled records.
- Off-screen developments are driven by scheduled mechanisms (faction clocks at downtime, Mythic random events drawing from lists, Pendragon family/economy tables at winter) rather than ad hoc invention, which is what makes them auditable.

### Gaps
- Mythic 2e page numbers and the exact Chaos Factor adjustment rule were not retrievable.
- No fetched source stated what experienced players *deliberately* stop tracking beyond dropping resolved threads; this is inferred.

## Key question 4: What have LLM-driven implementations of Mythic, Ironsworn or similar reported about the model honouring oracle/ruling results versus overriding them?

### Takeaway
Practitioner and open-source reports agree that an unconstrained chat model "fudges every roll in your favour", cannot produce uniform random numbers, and loses state; the working mitigations are to move dice, arithmetic, state and XP into deterministic code, constrain the model's decision output with schemas, and prompt it to declare difficulty before rolling and apply results without softening.

### Cited Findings
- Cyb3rRon1n/oracle (fetched README): "The server owns all truth. Character sheets, world state, turn order, the campaign clock — every mechanical number is computed by the Python engine." Two-phase turns: "Constrained JSON can't malform; unconstrained prose doesn't flatten." "On the Ollama backend the decide phase runs under llama.cpp structured output; malformed tool calls are physically impossible, not merely discouraged." "Real XP and leveling, awarded deterministically." Tool calls are requests the engine validates, not grants of authority. — [Cyb3rRon1n/oracle](https://github.com/Cyb3rRon1n/oracle)
- josbell/ironsworn-ai-experiment (fetched README): the AI is limited to oracle interpretation ("weave random oracle results into the narrative scene"), NPC generation and session recaps; "NPC and event generators instruct the model to return JSON only, then validate the response against a Zod schema"; oracles remain "random table rolled for narrative input"; the README does not state safeguards against the model reinterpreting rolls — [josbell/ironsworn-ai-experiment](https://github.com/josbell/ironsworn-ai-experiment)
- gcoulby/IronswornCompanion contains no AI features; oracles are deterministic table rolls — [gcoulby/IronswornCompanion](https://github.com/gcoulby/IronswornCompanion)
- Practitioner reports (search extracts): ChatGPT "narrates beautifully but fudges every roll in your favor" when untamed; "ChatGPT doesn't enforce game mechanics ... the outcome is whatever the AI decides sounds good, with no real dice rolls"; "When ChatGPT 'rolls' dice, it's producing a plausible looking number from its training data, not a uniform random number—research found that language models produce numbers that look random but deviate significantly from true randomness"; recommended fix: declare "a D20 check and difficulty before any risky action and apply the rolled result honestly, never softening outcomes for story convenience", with character-sheet maintenance in the prompt — [BladeRPG, ChatGPT as Your DM: Copy-Paste Prompt, Dice Rules & the Three Walls](https://www.bladerpg.com/en/chatgpt-dm/); [Dungeons Deep, Can ChatGPT Run D&D and Tabletop RPGs? 2026](https://dungeonsdeep.ai/blog/playing-dd-and-ttrpgs-with-chatgpt-an-honest-look-at-what-works-and-what-breaks); [Feathered Fiction, Solo TTRPGs with AI as GM (Jan 2026)](https://featheredfiction.com/2026/01/05/solo-rpg-ai-gm-guide/); [Oracle-RPG, Solo DM Guide Part 3: ChatGPT as AI DM (2023)](https://oracle-rpg.com/2023/03/solo-dm-guide-part-3-chatgpt-as-assistant-ai-dungeon-master/)
- Academic: "Static Vs. Agentic Game Master AI for Facilitating Solo Role-Playing Experiences" (arXiv 2502.19519, Feb 2025) and "You Have Thirteen Hours in Which to Solve the Labyrinth" (arXiv 2409.06949, Sept 2024) exist on LLM game masters for solo/tabletop play; abstracts could not be fetched (arxiv blocked), so no findings are reported here — [arXiv 2502.19519](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19519); [arXiv 2409.06949](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06949v1)
- A GitHub repository search for "mythic game master emulator LLM" / "mythic gme llm" returned zero repositories — [GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=mythic+gme+llm&type=repositories)

### Inferences
- The two open-source projects that were fully read take opposite ends of a spectrum: oracle (engine owns everything, model narrates) and ironsworn-ai-experiment (model only interprets already-rolled oracles). Both keep randomness out of the model; neither reports a measured override rate.
- The prompt-only approach (declare DC first, apply honestly) relies on model compliance and is the weakest safeguard reported; the repository's own "code checks required evidence" design matches the engine-owned-truth pattern.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of how often LLMs override oracle/dice results was found; the two arXiv papers likely contain relevant data but were inaccessible.
- No LLM implementation of Mythic GME specifically was found on GitHub; Mythic + LLM practice appears to live in blog/Reddit posts that were blocked.

## Key question 5: What review cadence and content do experienced solo players recommend?

### Takeaway
Published systems fix the cadence to the fiction (end of scene for lists; per downtime/score for clocks and training; per season or per year for advancement, aging, economy and family), while journaling advice recommends a short pre-session skim plus per-session scene/choice/thread notes and a running timeline; no source recommended a "every N sessions" review as such.

### Cited Findings
- Mythic 2e: bookkeeping at the end of every scene (lists + Chaos Factor) (search extract) — [Mythic GME 2e Adventure Lists](https://www.scribd.com/document/693163042/Mythic-GME-2e-Adventure-Lists)
- Mythras: experience rolls "at the end of every successful scenario or storyline; or after perhaps two or three sessions of play" (search extract) — [Mythras SRD](https://github.com/raleel/mythras-srd/blob/main/0004_Game_System.md)
- Blades: training, projects and faction clocks each downtime (after every score) — [Blades SRD mirror](https://github.com/amazingrando/blades-in-the-dark-srd-content/blob/main/Blades-in-the-Dark-SRD.md)
- Ars Magica: every season (three months) (search extract) — [Project Redcap ch. 10](https://www.redcap.org/page/Ars_Magica_5E_Standard_Edition,_Chapter_Ten:_Long-Term_Events)
- Pendragon: annually, nine steps — [DavidTPate/pendragon](https://github.com/DavidTPate/pendragon)
- Journaling: skim notes at session start; per-session scenes/choices/threads; timeline spread of major in-world events (search extracts) — [Soloist](https://soloist.substack.com/p/8-journaling-tips-for-solo-rpgs); [Making Mythology](https://makingmythology.substack.com/p/solo-rpg-journalingeasier-than-you); [Castle Grief](https://castlegrief.substack.com/p/6-fixes-for-your-solo-ttrpg-campaign)

### Inferences
- A tenth-turn or 30-day review (as in the repository contract) sits between Blades' per-downtime and Ars Magica's per-season cadence and is consistent with practice; its content should mirror Pendragon's checklist shape (experience, aging/condition, economics, relationships/family, off-screen events).

### Gaps
- The Castle Grief "6 fixes" article and Soloist posts could not be fetched, so their exact recommendations on review cadence are unknown beyond extracts.
